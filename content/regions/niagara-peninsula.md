## Overview
The Niagara Peninsula, in southern Ontario between Lake Ontario and Lake Erie, is Canada's largest wine appellation by vineyard area and the centre of its Icewine industry. It is regulated under the Vintners Quality Alliance (VQA) system.

## Typical grapes
Riesling and Chardonnay are the leading whites, while Cabernet Franc, Pinot Noir and Merlot are important reds. Vidal, a hybrid, is widely used for Icewine, alongside Riesling and Cabernet Franc.

## Wine styles
Icewine is intensely sweet and high in acidity, with flavours of apricot, honey and tropical fruit. Table wines include crisp dry and off-dry Riesling, restrained Chardonnay, and medium-bodied reds with lively acidity.

## What makes it special
Lake Ontario moderates temperatures, and breezes off the lake circulate against the Niagara Escarpment, reducing the risk of winter cold damage and spring frost. Icewine must be made from grapes naturally frozen on the vine; under VQA rules they are harvested and pressed while the air temperature is -8 C or colder, and the juice must reach at least 35 Brix, so yields are tiny. The Niagara Peninsula is a designated appellation within Ontario, containing two regional appellations, Niagara-on-the-Lake and Niagara Escarpment, and ten sub-appellations that reflect soils and proximity to the lake, from the clay and limestone benches below the escarpment to the flatter lakeshore.
