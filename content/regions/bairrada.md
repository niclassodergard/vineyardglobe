## Overview
Bairrada is a DOC in central-western Portugal, between Aveiro and Coimbra, close to the Atlantic coast. It was demarcated as a DOC in 1979. It is known for tannic, age-worthy Baga reds and for a large share of Portugal's sparkling wine.

## Typical grapes
Baga is the dominant red, small-berried, thick-skinned and high in tannin and acidity; wines labelled Bairrada Clássico must contain at least half Baga. Touriga Nacional and other reds are also permitted. White grapes include Maria Gomes (Fernão Pires), Bical and Arinto, with Cercial, Rabo de Ovelha and Chardonnay also allowed.

## Wine styles
Baga makes structured reds that need time, with red fruit, earth and savoury notes. Whites are fresh and lively. Espumante, made by the traditional method from Baga and white grapes, is a regional speciality, with Anadia a centre of production.

## What makes it special
Vineyards sit mainly on clay and clay-limestone soils, with some sand, and the mild, damp Atlantic climate keeps acidity high. Autumn rain is a recurring risk for late-ripening Baga, whose tight bunches are prone to rot, so careful vineyard work matters. Producers range from traditionalists to modern estates, and the region is famous for leitão, roast suckling pig, a classic match for its sparkling wines.
