## Overview
The Willamette Valley in northwestern Oregon is the state's leading wine region and one of the world's most respected sources of New World Pinot Noir. It lies at a similar latitude to Burgundy, a little further south.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir is the flagship. Chardonnay (including Dijon clones) has grown in reputation, and Pinot Gris is widely planted and a major white. Pinot Blanc and Riesling are also grown.

## Wine styles
Pinot Noir is typically elegant and red-fruited with good acidity and earthy, spicy notes. Whites are fresh and medium-bodied, and traditional-method sparkling wine is growing.

## What makes it special
A cool, rainy maritime climate with dry summers and the Coast Range shielding the valley suits slow ripening. Sub-AVAs differ by soil: volcanic Jory in the Dundee Hills, marine sedimentary soils in the Coast Range foothills and windblown loess elsewhere. Oregon's pioneering 1960s growers, strong environmental ethos and strict labelling rules, including a minimum 90 percent varietal content for Pinot Noir, define its identity.
