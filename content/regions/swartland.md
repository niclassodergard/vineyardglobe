## Overview
Swartland lies north of Cape Town in the Western Cape. Once known mainly for wheat and bulk wine, it has become the centre of South Africa's quality-driven new wave since the early 2000s.

## Typical grapes
Chenin Blanc is the main white and often comes from old bush vines. Syrah, Grenache, Carignan, Cinsault and Mourvèdre make Rhône-style reds and blends, and other varieties are planted in smaller amounts.

## Wine styles
Chenin Blanc ranges from fresh to rich and textural. Reds tend to be fresher and less extracted than traditional South African styles, with spicy, savoury fruit. Many producers favour low intervention winemaking, using wild yeasts and little new oak.

## What makes it special
Dry-farmed old bush vines, many decades old, grow on ancient shale, granite and iron-rich soils, yielding low crops. The Swartland Independent Producers association, launched around 2010-2011, set a quality code that restricts additions and stresses terroir expression. The warm, dry climate is moderated by cool Atlantic influences in places.
