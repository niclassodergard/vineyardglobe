## Overview
Constantia is a Wine of Origin ward on the Cape Peninsula, in the southern suburbs of Cape Town and within the Cape Town district. Its first estate was granted in 1685, making it South Africa's oldest wine area still in production. The neighbouring Hout Bay and Cape Point wards are separate appellations.

## Typical grapes
Sauvignon Blanc is the leading variety and Sémillon is an important historic white. Muscat de Frontignan (Muscat à Petits Grains) makes the sweet wines. Reds include Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah, often in blends.

## Wine styles
Sauvignon Blanc here is tense and mineral, with green fruit, citrus and herbal notes, and is often ranked among the Cape's best. Sémillon can be rich and age-worthy. Sweet Vin de Constance, an unfortified wine made from late-harvested, partly raisined Muscat, is golden, honeyed and remarkably fresh. Reds are elegant and moderate in alcohol.

## What makes it special
Vineyards sit on the south-eastern slopes of the Constantiaberg behind Table Mountain, where decomposed granite soils and cooling breezes from False Bay slow ripening. Constantia's sweet wine was prized across 18th and 19th century Europe, and the style was revived at Klein Constantia in the 1980s. Groot Constantia, the original estate founded by Governor Simon van der Stel, now anchors the area's long history as a museum and working winery.
