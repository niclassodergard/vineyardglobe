## Overview
The Northern Rhône is a narrow strip of mostly steep, terraced vineyards along the Rhône river between Vienne and Valence. Its appellations are small and prestigious, and quite separate from the larger southern Rhône.

## Typical grapes
Syrah is the only red grape of the region. Most red appellations allow a small share of white grapes in the blend: Viognier in Côte-Rôtie, and Marsanne or Roussanne in Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage and Saint-Joseph, while Cornas must be pure Syrah. Viognier is the sole white of Condrieu and Château-Grillet, while Marsanne and Roussanne are the whites of Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph and Saint-Péray.

## Wine styles
Red wines range from the perfumed, floral Côte-Rôtie to the powerful, savoury Cornas and Hermitage, with black pepper, olive and dark fruit notes. Condrieu gives rich, peachy and floral dry whites, and Hermitage blanc is full and waxy. Crozes-Hermitage and Saint-Joseph are generally more approachable. Saint-Péray also makes traditional-method sparkling wine.

## What makes it special
Granite slopes, some so steep they need terraces held by dry-stone walls, define the best sites. Côte-Rôtie is divided into the Côte Brune and Côte Blonde, and up to 20 percent Viognier may be co-fermented with Syrah there. Hermitage hill is the historic centre, and Château-Grillet is a single-estate appellation, one of the smallest in France.
