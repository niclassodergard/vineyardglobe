## Overview
The Yarra Valley lies east of Melbourne in Victoria. Vines were first planted in the 1830s, declined in the early 20th century, and were replanted from the 1960s, since when it has become a leading cool-climate region.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir and Chardonnay are the key varieties, used for both still and sparkling wines. Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah (Shiraz in Australia) also feature, with Syrah often made in a peppery, Rhône-like cool-climate style.

## Wine styles
Pinot Noir is elegant and perfumed, ranging from bright and red-fruited to more structured. Chardonnay is typically restrained, citrusy and mineral, with careful oak use. Sparkling wines made by the traditional method benefit from the cool conditions, and Cabernet is usually leaner and more medium-bodied.

## What makes it special
Altitude and varied aspects create strong differences between sub-regions: the Upper Yarra is cooler and higher, with young, deep red volcanic soils, while the warmer Lower Yarra has older, less fertile grey sandy and clay loams. The region's move towards restraint and finesse, and its closeness to Melbourne, have made it highly influential in modern Australian wine.
