## Overview
Naoussa is a PDO in Imathia, in Greek Macedonia, on the eastern slopes of Mount Vermio. It is widely regarded as the benchmark home of Xinomavro and was among Greece's first legally defined appellations, recognised in 1971.

## Typical grapes
Xinomavro, whose name means "acid black", is the only variety permitted for Naoussa PDO wines. It is high in acidity and tannin, and it is often compared with Nebbiolo for its combination of firm tannin, high acidity and a relatively light colour that turns brick-orange with age.

## Wine styles
The PDO covers only red wines made from 100% Xinomavro. Labels may indicate Reserve and Grande Reserve, which require longer minimum ageing in barrel and bottle before release. Young wines show red cherry, tomato, olive and dried herb notes, developing leather, spice and dried fruit with age. Wines that fall outside the PDO rules, such as blends and Xinomavro rosé, are sold under regional designations like Imathia or Macedonia PGI.

## What makes it special
Most vineyards sit on the south-eastern foothills of Vermio at roughly 150 to 400 metres, on a mix of clay, limestone and sandy soils. Cool nights and the mountain setting help preserve acidity. The wines reward cellaring, and a younger generation of growers is exploring single-vineyard bottlings and different soils and aspects.
