## Overview
Chablis is the northernmost district of Burgundy, around the town of Chablis in the Yonne département, much closer to Champagne's Aube vineyards than to the Côte d'Or. It produces only white wine, all from Chardonnay.

## Typical grapes
Chardonnay is the sole grape. Here (locally also known historically as Beaunois) it ripens with high acidity and moderate alcohol thanks to the cool climate.

## Wine styles
The appellation is a four-tier hierarchy: Petit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru and Chablis Grand Cru. Wines are dry, taut and citrusy, with green apple, lemon and a flinty, saline or chalky character. Many are fermented and aged in stainless steel or old oak to preserve purity, while Grand Cru wines often show more weight and can age for many years.

## What makes it special
Most vineyards sit on Kimmeridgian soils, a limestone and marl rich in fossilised small oyster shells, which many growers credit for the wines' minerality; Petit Chablis comes mainly from the younger Portlandian limestone on the plateaus. Spring frost is a serious threat, and growers use heaters, sprinklers or wind machines to protect the buds. The seven Grand Cru climats (Blanchot, Bougros, Les Clos, Grenouilles, Preuses, Valmur and Vaudésir) form a single south-west-facing slope on the right bank of the Serein river above the town, and Premier Cru sites such as Montée de Tonnerre, Fourchaume and Vaillons are named climats too.
