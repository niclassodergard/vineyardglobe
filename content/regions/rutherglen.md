## Overview
Rutherglen is a Geographical Indication in north-east Victoria, on the Murray River near the New South Wales border. It is Australia's most famous source of fortified wines, and vineyards spread here in the decades after the gold rush of the 1860s. Its neighbours in North East Victoria include the separate Glenrowan, Beechworth and King Valley GIs.

## Typical grapes
Rutherglen Muscat is made from a dark-skinned form of Muscat à Petits Grains, locally called Brown Muscat. Rutherglen Topaque, formerly labelled Tokay, is made from Muscadelle. The region is also known for full-bodied Durif (Petite Sirah) and Shiraz table wines.

## Wine styles
Grapes are picked very ripe, often with some raisining, and fermentation is stopped by adding grape spirit to leave high residual sugar. The wines are then aged in old oak casks, often in hot sheds, and blended across many vintages in solera-style systems. Muscat shows raisin, toffee, rose and spice, while Topaque is more tea-leaf, malt and butterscotch. Both are rich and sweet, with surprising freshness.

## What makes it special
Rutherglen has its own four-tier classification for these fortifieds, Rutherglen, Classic, Grand and Rare, which reflects increasing age, richness and complexity. Rare wines can contain components many decades old, with extraordinary depth. Warm, dry autumns allow late harvests, and the region is home to a close-knit community of long-established family producers.
