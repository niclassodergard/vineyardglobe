## Overview
Rueda is a Denominación de Origen in Castilla y León, on the high plateau south of Valladolid and along the Duero river. It is Spain's leading white wine region.

## Typical grapes
Verdejo is the signature grape and the dominant one. Sauvignon Blanc is widely planted and often blended with it, and Viura (Macabeo) is also permitted, along with a few other varieties such as Palomino and, more recently, Chardonnay and Viognier.

## Wine styles
Verdejo gives aromatic, dry whites with citrus, fennel, herbal and stone fruit notes, and a characteristic slightly bitter finish. Wines labelled with a single grape variety must contain at least 85 percent of it, and blends labelled simply Rueda are based on Verdejo and/or Sauvignon Blanc. The rules introduced around 2020 also added Gran Vino de Rueda, from vines at least 30 years old with lower yields, and village wines (Vino de Pueblo). Some wines are barrel fermented or aged on lees, and sparkling Rueda Espumoso is also made.

## What makes it special
Vineyards lie at roughly 700 to 800 metres, giving hot days and cool nights that preserve acidity. Soils are stony, sandy or limestone-rich, and some old, ungrafted bush vines survive on sandy sites because the phylloxera louse struggles in sand. Night harvesting is common to keep the grapes cool and fresh. The region's modern identity dates from the 1970s, when Rioja's Marqués de Riscal began making fresh Verdejo here, and Rueda became Castilla y León's first DO in 1980.
