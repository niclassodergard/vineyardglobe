## Overview
Alto Adige, or Südtirol, is Italy's northernmost wine region, in the Alps bordering Austria and Switzerland, with vineyards mainly along the Adige and Isarco valleys. It is bilingual (Italian and German), and cooperatives produce the majority of its wine.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Blanc (Weissburgunder), Pinot Gris (Pinot Grigio), Gewürztraminer and Sauvignon Blanc lead among whites. Lagrein, Schiava (Vernatsch) and Pinot Noir are the key reds. Gewürztraminer is thought to take its name from the village of Tramin.

## Wine styles
Whites are crisp, aromatic and mineral, with bright acidity. Schiava gives light, fruity reds, while Lagrein is deeply coloured and fuller, with plum, dark berry and chocolate notes. Terlano whites, often Pinot Blanc-based blends, can age well. Sparkling wines are made by the traditional method.

## What makes it special
Vines grow on steep slopes and valley floors from roughly 200 to over 1,000 metres, where warm days and cold nights preserve aromatics and acidity. Soils vary widely, from porphyry and dolomitic limestone to alluvial and glacial deposits. Alto Adige (Südtirol) DOC covers the region, with named sub-zones such as Terlano, Santa Maddalena, Valle Isarco and Val Venosta; Kalterersee (Lago di Caldaro), a light Schiava red, has its own DOC. Quality cooperatives and a focus on varietal purity define its reputation.
