## Overview
The Sherry Triangle in Andalusia, southern Spain, is formed by Jerez de la Frontera, Sanlúcar de Barrameda and El Puerto de Santa María. It is the home of Sherry, one of the world's great fortified wines, produced under the Jerez-Xérès-Sherry DO.

## Typical grapes
Palomino is by far the main grape, producing neutral base wines that suit biological and oxidative ageing. Pedro Ximénez is dried in the sun to make intensely sweet wines, and Moscatel is used in smaller amounts.

## Wine styles
Fino and Manzanilla are pale, dry and saline, aged under a layer of flor yeast. Amontillado begins under flor and then ages oxidatively, while Oloroso is aged oxidatively, giving rich, nutty wines. Pedro Ximénez and cream sherries are sweet.

## What makes it special
Sherry is aged in a solera system of fractionally blended barrels. Albariza, a chalky white soil, retains winter moisture for the dry summers. Manzanilla must be aged in Sanlúcar, where cooler, humid conditions favour flor. Flor-aged sherries are fortified to only about 15% so the yeast can survive and are best drunk fresh, while oxidative styles are fortified higher and can age for decades.
