## Overview
Mendoza, in the foothills of the Andes, produces most of Argentina's wine and is the world's leading source of Malbec. The vineyards are among the highest in the world, from about 700 to over 1,500 metres.

## Typical grapes
Malbec is the signature grape. Cabernet Sauvignon, Bonarda and other reds are also grown, and Chardonnay is the leading quality white, with Torrontés and old-vine Sémillon also grown.

## Wine styles
Malbec ranges from juicy, plummy and approachable to deep, structured and age-worthy wines from top sites. Luján de Cuyo and the Uco Valley are the best-known areas. Fresh, mineral Chardonnay from high Uco sites has gained recognition.

## What makes it special
The climate is desert-like, with very little rainfall, so vines depend on irrigation from Andean snowmelt, historically through channels built by indigenous peoples and early settlers. Altitude gives intense sunlight, large day-night temperature swings and good acidity. In Uco, alluvial soils with calcareous layers add to the diversity and have driven interest in single-vineyard and sub-zone bottlings.
