## Overview
Campania lies around Naples in southern Italy and is one of the country's most historic wine regions, known to the Romans for Falernian wine. Its quality wines come largely from the hills of the interior, notably Irpinia.

## Typical grapes
Aglianico is the great red, and Piedirosso is also grown. The leading whites are Fiano, Greco and Falanghina, all ancient varieties that have been revived in recent decades.

## Wine styles
Taurasi, made from Aglianico, is powerful, tannic and acidic with dark fruit, spice and long ageing potential. It became a DOCG in 1993, the first in southern Italy, and Fiano di Avellino, Greco di Tufo and Aglianico del Taburno followed. Taurasi must be aged for at least three years, four for Riserva. Fiano is waxy and nutty with orchard fruit, Greco is structured and mineral, and Falanghina is fresh and citrusy.

## What makes it special
Volcanic influence from Vesuvius and ancient volcanic and tuffaceous soils shape many wines. Inland vineyards in Irpinia sit at altitude, giving cooler nights and slow ripening that preserve acidity. Some old vines survive ungrafted on volcanic sand, such as in the Campi Flegrei, and the region's native varieties make it a showcase of southern Italian identity.
