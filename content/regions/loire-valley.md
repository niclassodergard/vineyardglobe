## Overview
The Loire stretches along France's longest river from the Atlantic coast to the centre of the country. It is one of France's most diverse wine regions in climate and style.

## Typical grapes
Melon de Bourgogne makes Muscadet near the coast. Chenin Blanc is the star of Anjou, Saumur, Vouvray and Montlouis, in styles from dry to sweet to sparkling. Sauvignon Blanc dominates Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé, and Cabernet Franc makes the reds of Chinon and Bourgueil.

## Wine styles
Expect high-acid, fresh whites, often aged on the lees in Muscadet sur lie. Chenin can be honeyed and long-lived, such as in Vouvray or Coteaux du Layon. Crémant de Loire and Saumur are sparkling wines, and Cabernet Franc reds are herbal and light to medium-bodied. Rosé d'Anjou is also made.

## What makes it special
Soils vary from limestone tuffeau and flint silex to schist, giving distinct profiles. The central valley between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes, famed for its châteaux, is a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape. Many producers follow organic and biodynamic approaches, and Chenin's versatility makes it a favourite of sommeliers.
