## Overview
Lodi is an AVA in California's Central Valley, spanning northern San Joaquin County and southern Sacramento County, south of the city of Sacramento. It is a large, diverse grape-growing area, established as an AVA in 1986, and best known for Zinfandel, of which it grows a large share of California's crop.

## Typical grapes
Zinfandel is the star, with some own-rooted old vines dating back more than a century, many on the sandy soils of the west side. Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah and Merlot are widely grown, along with Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc. Many other varieties, including Rhône and Iberian grapes, are planted.

## Wine styles
Old-vine Zinfandel is rich, ripe and spicy with dark and red fruit. Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot are generous and soft, and Rhône-style blends and dry whites are also made.

## What makes it special
The Mediterranean climate has hot days cooled by evening breezes from the San Francisco Bay and Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta. Seven sub-AVAs, approved in 2006, recognise differences in soil, topography and climate, from the deep sandy soils of Mokelumne River, the largest, to the higher, rolling eastern hills of Clements Hills and Borden Ranch, which feel less of the Delta's cooling influence.
