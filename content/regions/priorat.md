## Overview
Priorat is a small mountainous region in Catalonia, south-west of Barcelona. It is one of only two Spanish regions awarded DOQ status, the other being Rioja (DOCa in Spanish).

## Typical grapes
Garnacha (Grenache) and Cariñena (Carignan) from old vines are the heart of the region, often supplemented by Syrah, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot. White wines from Garnacha Blanca and Macabeo are a small share.

## Wine styles
Reds are dense, concentrated and mineral, with dark fruit, spice and high alcohol, balanced by freshness. Wines are often labelled by village or vineyard.

## What makes it special
The signature soil is llicorella, a dark slate and quartz that forces roots deep and keeps yields very low. Steep terraces make work largely manual. The modern revival began in the late 1980s, led by pioneers such as René Barbier, Álvaro Palacios and Daphne Glorian, who brought international attention to old vineyards. Priorat has a site-based pyramid: DOQ Priorat, Vi de Vila (village), Vi de Paratge, Vinya Classificada and Gran Vinya Classificada.
