## Overview
Marlborough, at the northeast tip of the South Island, is New Zealand's largest wine region and accounts for the majority of the country's vineyard area. Its Sauvignon Blanc, first planted in the 1970s, brought New Zealand international acclaim from the mid-1980s.

## Typical grapes
Sauvignon Blanc dominates plantings by a wide margin. Pinot Noir is the main red, while Chardonnay and Pinot Gris are the other important varieties, with some Riesling and Gewürztraminer also grown.

## Wine styles
Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc is intensely aromatic, with passion fruit, gooseberry, citrus and fresh-cut herb notes and high, zesty acidity. Some producers make more textured, barrel-fermented or wild-yeast versions. Pinot Noir and traditional-method sparkling wines are also important.

## What makes it special
Long sunshine hours, cool nights and low rainfall create a long, slow ripening season that preserves aromatics. The Wairau Valley has stony, free-draining alluvial soils, while the Awatere Valley is cooler, drier and windier, giving more herbal, mineral wines. The Southern Valleys have heavier clay soils, favouring Pinot Noir.
