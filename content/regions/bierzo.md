## Overview
Bierzo is a Denominación de Origen, created in 1989, in the northwest of Castilla y León, a valley ringed by mountains near the border with Galicia. It has become one of Spain's most exciting red wine regions since the late 1990s.

## Typical grapes
Mencía is the main red grape, making up the large majority of plantings. Godello and Doña Blanca are the main white grapes, and Garnacha Tintorera (Alicante Bouschet) is also permitted.

## Wine styles
Mencía produces fresh, perfumed reds with red and black fruit, floral and sometimes slightly herbal or mineral notes. Styles range from young, unoaked wines to structured ones from old vines aged in oak. Godello makes dry, textured whites with citrus and stone fruit. Since 2017 Bierzo has also had a Burgundy-inspired classification by origin, with tiers such as Vino de Villa (village wine), Vino de Paraje (named site) and classified single vineyards.

## What makes it special
The bowl-shaped valley is sheltered from the Atlantic by mountains, giving a mix of Atlantic and continental influences. The best vineyards are on steep slopes of slate and quartzite, while valley floors have clay and alluvial soils that tend to give simpler wines. Many old bush vines survive, and producers such as Descendientes de J. Palacios, founded by Álvaro Palacios and his nephew Ricardo Pérez in the late 1990s, helped bring Bierzo international attention.
