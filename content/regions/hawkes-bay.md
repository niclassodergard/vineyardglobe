## Overview
Hawke's Bay, on the east coast of the North Island, is one of New Zealand's oldest wine regions, with winemaking dating to the 1850s (Mission Estate is the country's oldest surviving winery), and its second-largest by area. It is warmer and drier than most of the country, which makes it the leader for quality red wines.

## Typical grapes
Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon are blended in Bordeaux style, and Syrah has become a regional specialty. Chardonnay is the leading white, alongside Sauvignon Blanc, with Pinot Gris and Viognier in smaller quantities.

## Wine styles
Reds are medium to full-bodied, with ripe fruit and soft, polished tannins. Syrah is peppery and perfumed, closer to the northern Rhône than to Australia. Chardonnay is among New Zealand's best, ranging from fresh and citrusy to richer, barrel-fermented styles.

## What makes it special
The Gimblett Gravels is a sub-region defined by its stony, free-draining former riverbed soils, which reflect heat and restrict vine vigour, and it sets the standard for the region's reds. Its warm, sunny climate, tempered by sea breezes, gives dependable ripening.
