## Overview
Waipara Valley lies in North Canterbury on New Zealand's South Island, roughly an hour north of Christchurch. Modern commercial viticulture began in the early 1980s, and the valley is now one of the country's most distinctive cool-climate areas.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir is the leading red and Riesling the signature white. Chardonnay, Pinot Gris and Sauvignon Blanc are also widely planted.

## Wine styles
Pinot Noir ranges from fragrant and red-fruited to structured and savoury. Riesling is made from bone dry to off-dry and sweet, with citrus and floral character and firm acidity. Chardonnay and aromatic whites round out the picture.

## What makes it special
The Teviotdale Hills shelter the valley from cool easterly winds off the Pacific, giving a dry growing season with warm days and cool nights; the valley is warmer than much of Canterbury, helped by dry north-westerly winds. Soils vary from free-draining gravels on the valley floor to limestone-influenced clay on the hill slopes, a combination often credited for the region's Pinot Noir and Riesling. Waipara Valley is a registered New Zealand Geographical Indication within the wider North Canterbury region.
