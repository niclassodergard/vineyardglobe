## Overview
Salta in northwest Argentina includes some of the world's highest commercial vineyards, in the Calchaquí Valleys. Cafayate is the best-known centre, at roughly 1,700 metres, and other sites rise considerably higher.

## Typical grapes
Torrontés (Riojano), a natural cross of Muscat of Alexandria and Criolla Chica, is the signature white. Malbec is the main red, with Cabernet Sauvignon and Tannat also grown.

## Wine styles
Torrontés is aromatic and floral, with peach and rose notes, and is usually dry with fresh acidity. Malbec here tends to be deeply coloured, intense and structured, with firm tannins. Tannat is a distinctive local red.

## What makes it special
Extreme altitude brings intense ultraviolet light, strong sun, big temperature swings and low rainfall, so the vines rely on irrigation. Grapes develop thick skins, giving concentrated colour and flavour while keeping acidity. The remote valleys, sandy alluvial soils and a long history of grape growing since Spanish colonial times set it apart from Mendoza. Some producers, such as Colomé, farm vineyards well above 2,000 metres.
