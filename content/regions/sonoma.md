## Overview
Sonoma County lies west of Napa in northern California and is more diverse in geography and style, stretching from the Pacific coast inland. It has many AVAs and a more varied, less uniform identity than its neighbour.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir and Chardonnay thrive in the cool, fog-influenced west. Zinfandel is a heritage grape, notably in Dry Creek Valley. Cabernet Sauvignon ripens well in warmer Alexander Valley, and Sauvignon Blanc is widely planted.

## Wine styles
Russian River and Sonoma Coast produce elegant, red-fruited Pinot Noir and balanced Chardonnay. Dry Creek Zinfandel is ripe and peppery, sometimes from old vines. Alexander Valley Cabernet is generally softer and plusher than Napa's. Carneros, shared with Napa, contributes to sparkling wine.

## What makes it special
Pacific fog and coastal winds cool the west, while inland valleys are warmer, giving a wide range of styles in one county. Many family-owned estates, old-vine field blends and a long Italian-American winemaking heritage shape its character, with a strong emphasis on sustainable farming.
