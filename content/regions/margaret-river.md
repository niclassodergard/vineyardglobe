## Overview
Margaret River sits on a narrow strip of coast in the far south-west of Western Australia, bordered by the Indian and Southern Oceans. Commercial winemaking began in the late 1960s, and the region is now renowned for quality out of proportion to its size.

## Typical grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon is the star red, often blended with Merlot in a Bordeaux style. Chardonnay is the leading white and is among Australia's most acclaimed. Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon, often blended, produce zesty, textured dry whites.

## Wine styles
Cabernet-based reds are structured and cassis-scented with fine tannins and notable ageing potential. Chardonnay ranges from tight and mineral to richer, barrel-fermented styles. SSB (Sémillon Sauvignon Blanc) blends are a regional specialty, with herbal freshness and body.

## What makes it special
The maritime climate is notably moderate, with few temperature extremes and dry summers that limit disease pressure. Gravelly loam soils over ancient granite and gneiss, much of it low in fertility, help yield balanced wines. The Wilyabrup sub-region is especially prized for Cabernet, and the region is known for a quality-first, boutique approach.
