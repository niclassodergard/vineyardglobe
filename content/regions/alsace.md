## Overview
Alsace lies between the Vosges mountains and the Rhine on France's eastern border. The Vosges create a rain shadow, making it one of France's driest and sunniest regions. German influence is clear in its culture and bottle shape.

## Typical grapes
The four "noble" grapes, traditionally the ones permitted for Grand Cru wines, are Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris and Muscat. Pinot Blanc and Sylvaner are also grown, and Pinot Noir is the main red. Wines are labelled by grape variety, which is unusual in France.

## Wine styles
Most wines are dry, aromatic whites from fresh, mineral Riesling to opulent, spicy Gewürztraminer. Vendange Tardive and Sélection de Grains Nobles are sweeter late-harvest styles. Crémant d'Alsace is the sparkling wine.

## What makes it special
Alsace has 51 Grand Cru vineyards, each with its own geology, and soils include granite, limestone, marl and sandstone. The Route des Vins links picturesque villages. Its tall, slender flûte bottles and varietal labelling make the region easy to learn.
