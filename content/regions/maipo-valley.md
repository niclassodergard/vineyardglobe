## Overview
The Maipo Valley is Chile's most historic fine-wine region, around and south of Santiago, and the heart of its Cabernet Sauvignon production. Many of Chile's best-known wineries are based here.

## Typical grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon is the star, particularly in Alto Maipo. Merlot and Carmenère are also planted, and Syrah, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc contribute in smaller amounts.

## Wine styles
Cabernet is typically full-bodied, with blackcurrant, mint and cedar notes, and elegant structure at its best. Blends combining Cabernet with Merlot, Carmenère or other grapes are common, along with some premium icon wines.

## What makes it special
The climate is Mediterranean, with warm, dry summers, irrigation from Andean rivers and cooling air from the mountains in the evenings. Alto Maipo, at higher altitude near the Andes, makes more concentrated and structured wines than the valley floor, and Puente Alto is the source of icon Cabernets such as Almaviva and Don Melchor. Phylloxera never took hold in Chile, so many vines are ungrafted, and a handful of 19th-century French-influenced estates shaped the region's reputation.
