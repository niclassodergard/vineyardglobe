## Overview
Serra Gaúcha, in the hills of the southern state of Rio Grande do Sul around Bento Gonçalves, is Brazil's main wine region and produces most of the country's wine. It was settled by Italian immigrants from 1875 onward, and their traditions still shape the culture and grape growing.

## Typical grapes
Merlot is the flagship red, with Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Tannat also important. Chardonnay, Riesling Itálico (Welschriesling) and Pinot Noir are the backbone of the sparkling wines, and Moscato grapes (Muscat) make the sweet, aromatic sparklers. Large areas are also planted with American and hybrid grapes such as Isabel (Isabella), used for juice and simple table wine.

## Wine styles
Sparkling wine is the region's calling card, from traditional-method brut and rosé to fruity tank-method wines and the sweet, aromatic Moscatel espumante. Reds such as Merlot are medium bodied and fresh, with blends and Tannat showing more structure. Whites are crisp and fruity.

## What makes it special
Many vineyards lie at roughly 600 to 800 metres in hilly country. Humidity and summer rainfall make disease pressure and ripening the main challenges, so producers manage canopies carefully and many train vines on overhead pergolas (latadas). Vale dos Vinhedos was Brazil's first wine Geographical Indication in 2002 and its first Denominação de Origem in 2012. It has since been followed by other indications, including Altos de Pinto Bandeira, a Denominação de Origem for traditional-method sparkling wine, which set rules on grapes and methods.
