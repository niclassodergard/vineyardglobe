## Overview
The Great Southern, in the south of Western Australia, is often described as Australia's largest wine region by area. It spans roughly 150 km north to south and 100 km east to west and has five official sub-regions.

## Typical grapes
Riesling is the flagship white, especially from Mount Barker and Frankland River. Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc do well, as do red varieties including Shiraz (Syrah), Cabernet Sauvignon and, in the cooler south, Pinot Noir.

## Wine styles
Riesling is dry, crisp and mineral with lime and citrus character, and ages well. Reds range from peppery, medium-bodied Shiraz to structured Cabernet. Cabernet Sauvignon here tends towards a firm, elegant, Bordeaux-like structure. Coastal areas around Denmark and Albany favour Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, including some sparkling wine.

## What makes it special
Sub-regions vary widely. Inland Frankland River has the largest vineyard area and is known for Shiraz, Cabernet and Riesling; Mount Barker is where the region's first commercial vineyard, Forest Hill, was planted in the mid-1960s; Porongurup, on ancient granite slopes, is known for fine Riesling; and coastal Albany and Denmark are wetter and cooler. The Southern Ocean tempers the climate.
