## Overview
The Barossa Valley, north-east of Adelaide in South Australia, is one of the country's oldest and most celebrated wine regions. Settled in the 1840s by German and English immigrants, it is home to some of the world's oldest producing vines.

## Typical grapes
Syrah, called Shiraz in Australia, is the flagship, making rich, plush reds. Grenache and Mourvèdre (Mataro locally) feature in GSM blends, and Cabernet Sauvignon is also significant. Sémillon and Riesling are the main whites, with Riesling at its best in the higher, cooler Eden Valley next door, which shares the wider Barossa zone.

## Wine styles
Barossa Shiraz is full-bodied, dark-fruited and spicy, often with chocolate notes and sometimes American or French oak influence. GSM blends are softer and more perfumed. Fortified wines and old-vine Grenache are also traditional, while Eden Valley Riesling is dry, lime-scented and age-worthy.

## What makes it special
South Australia has never been affected by phylloxera, thanks in part to strict quarantine, so many ancient, often dry-grown vines survive on their own roots, some well over a century old. The Barossa Old Vine Charter classifies vines by age, from Old Vine (35+ years) up to Ancestor Vine (125+ years). Warm, dry summers and a strong Germanic heritage shape its character.
