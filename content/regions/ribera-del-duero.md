## Overview
Ribera del Duero lies on the high Castilian plateau along the Duero river in north-central Spain, and became a DO in 1982. It is known for powerful, concentrated reds.

## Typical grapes
Tempranillo, locally called Tinto Fino or Tinta del País, is the main grape, and red wines must contain at least 75 percent of it. Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Malbec and Garnacha are permitted as minor partners. Albillo Mayor is the traditional white grape, and since 2019 the DO also covers white wines made from it.

## Wine styles
Reds are dark, structured and intense, with black fruit and firm tannin. Like Rioja, wines are labelled Joven, Crianza, Reserva or Gran Reserva by ageing. Rosado and, more recently, white Ribera are also made.

## What makes it special
Vineyards sit at roughly 700 to 1,000 metres, giving big day-night temperature swings that preserve acidity and colour. Spring frosts are a risk. Vega Sicilia, founded in the 19th century, helped build the region's prestige, and Pesquera raised its profile in the 1980s. Old bush vines are a significant resource.
