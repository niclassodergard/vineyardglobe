## Overview
Walker Bay is a coastal Wine of Origin district around Hermanus, in the Cape South Coast region roughly 100 km southeast of Cape Town. It has seven wards; the best known is the Hemel-en-Aarde ("Heaven and Earth") Valley area, divided into three wards: Hemel-en-Aarde Valley, Upper Hemel-en-Aarde Valley and Hemel-en-Aarde Ridge.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir and Chardonnay are the leading varieties, and the valley is among South Africa's best-known sources of both. Sauvignon Blanc is also planted, and some Syrah and other varieties appear elsewhere in the district.

## Wine styles
Pinot Noir is elegant, with red fruit, spice and earthy notes. Chardonnay ranges from lean and mineral to richer, barrel-fermented styles. Sauvignon Blanc is fresh and saline-tinged, shaped by the nearby sea.

## What makes it special
Cool breezes off the cold Atlantic waters of Walker Bay, only a few kilometres away, moderate the climate, giving long, cool ripening seasons that are unusual for the Cape. Soils matter greatly: the valley is known for stony, clay-rich soils derived from Bokkeveld shale, contrasted with sandier soils from Table Mountain sandstone and decomposed granite in other parts. Tim Hamilton Russell pioneered the area from the mid-1970s, and the district has since gained an international reputation for cool-climate Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.
