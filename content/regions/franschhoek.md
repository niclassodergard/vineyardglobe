## Overview
Franschhoek, "French corner", is a mountain-ringed valley in South Africa's Western Cape, near Stellenbosch and Paarl. French Huguenot refugees settled here from 1688 and brought viticultural knowledge. Long a ward of Paarl, Franschhoek Valley became a Wine of Origin district within the Coastal Region in 2010.

## Typical grapes
Sémillon is the heritage white: it once dominated Cape vineyards, and Franschhoek retains some of the country's oldest plantings, dating to the early 1900s. Chardonnay and Chenin Blanc are important whites; Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah lead the reds, with Pinot Noir and Chardonnay also used for sparkling wine.

## Wine styles
Cabernet-based blends, Syrah and Rhône-style reds sit alongside Sémillon, Chardonnay and Chenin Blanc. The valley is also a leading centre for Méthode Cap Classique, South Africa's bottle-fermented sparkling wine.

## What makes it special
Mountains surround the valley on three sides, shaping its Mediterranean climate and trapping or deflecting winds, while the Berg River rises in the mountains and runs down the valley. Soils range from sandy alluvium on the valley floor to sandstone- and granite-derived soils on the slopes. Its Huguenot heritage and old Sémillon vines give it a strong sense of history in South Africa's Cape winelands.
