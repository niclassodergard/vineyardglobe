## Overview
The Wachau is a narrow stretch of the Danube valley in Lower Austria, west of Vienna. Its terraced vineyards and historic towns make it one of Austria's best-known wine areas, and a UNESCO World Heritage landscape.

## Typical grapes
Grüner Veltliner and Riesling are the two key varieties. Grüner often shows white pepper, citrus and green-herb notes, while Riesling is more mineral and precise.

## Wine styles
The Vinea Wachau growers' association created its own quality scale based on the must weight and alcohol of dry wines: Steinfeder (lightest), Federspiel (medium) and Smaragd (richest, usually ripest and fullest). Most wines are dry, and since 2020 the Wachau DAC also classifies wines as regional, village or single-vineyard (Ried) wines.

## What makes it special
Steep stone-walled terraces of primary rock soils, including gneiss, and loess lower down, shape the wines. Warm Pannonian air from the east meets cool air from the northern forests, creating large day-night temperature differences that preserve acidity. Wines can age well, especially top Smaragd Rieslings.
