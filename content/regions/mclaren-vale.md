## Overview
McLaren Vale lies south of Adelaide in South Australia, between the Mount Lofty Ranges and the Gulf St Vincent coast. Vines were planted here in the 1830s, and the region has a strong tradition of sustainability and Mediterranean-style varieties.

## Typical grapes
Syrah (Shiraz in Australia) is the leading red, producing ripe, concentrated wines. Grenache, especially from old bush vines, has become a regional strength, and Cabernet Sauvignon also performs well. Among whites, Chardonnay is established, while Vermentino and other Mediterranean varieties are increasingly planted.

## Wine styles
Shiraz is generous and dark-fruited with notes of chocolate and licorice. Grenache is fragrant, juicy and softer in tannin. Blends of Grenache, Shiraz and Mourvèdre are common, along with crisp, savoury whites suited to the warm, dry climate.

## What makes it special
Sea breezes moderate the warm Mediterranean climate, and the region has remarkably diverse geology, from sandy soils to ironstone and limestone, which has helped it gain recognition for terroir-focused wines. Many growers have adopted organic and biodynamic practices, and several old-vine sites remain productive.
