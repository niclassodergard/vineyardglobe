## Overview
The Aconcagua Valley lies north of Santiago and takes its name from the Aconcagua River, which runs down from the Andes, past Mount Aconcagua, the highest peak in the Americas, to the Pacific. It is the namesake sub-region of Chile's wider Aconcagua Region, which also includes the Casablanca and San Antonio valleys, and is defined by a sharp contrast between a warm inland valley and a cool coast.

## Typical grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon is the flagship in the warm inland valley, alongside Syrah and Carmenère. Closer to the ocean, Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay take over, with Pinot Noir and cool-climate Syrah also grown in the coastal zone.

## Wine styles
Inland reds are ripe, concentrated and full-bodied, with dark fruit, firm tannins and often oak ageing, and many are blended. Coastal wines are fresher: crisp, citrusy Sauvignon Blanc, restrained Chardonnay and peppery Syrah with higher acidity.

## What makes it special
Since 2011 Chilean labels may add the terms Costa, Entre Cordilleras or Andes to show whether a vineyard is influenced by the coast, lies between the mountain ranges, or sits in the Andean foothills, and Aconcagua Costa is the best-known coastal example. Inland vineyards, such as those around Panquehue, enjoy abundant sunshine, little rain and Andean snowmelt irrigation, with large day-night temperature swings. Coastal sites are cooled by the Humboldt Current and morning fog, so one valley can produce both powerful reds and lean, high-acid whites. Viña Errázuriz, long based in Panquehue, is the valley's best-known producer.
