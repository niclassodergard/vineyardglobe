## Overview
Canelones, just north of Montevideo, is Uruguay's largest and most important wine department, producing the majority of the country's wine. Many of the country's long-established, often family-run wineries are located here.

## Typical grapes
Tannat, introduced to Uruguay from southwest France by the Basque settler Pascual Harriague around 1870, is the national grape and the most planted variety. Merlot and Cabernet Franc are also planted, along with Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay for whites.

## Wine styles
Tannat here is typically deep in colour with firm tannins, dark fruit and good acidity, often in a somewhat softer, fruitier style than in Madiran, its French homeland. It is made as a varietal wine and in blends with Merlot or Cabernet Franc. Fresh whites, rosé and sparkling wines are also produced.

## What makes it special
The climate is maritime and humid, with the Atlantic and the Río de la Plata moderating temperatures but bringing rain and humidity near harvest, so vineyard management and site selection matter. Soils are generally clay-rich with some limestone and are fertile, so yield control is important. Open canopy systems, such as the lyre trellis, are used by some growers to improve air flow. Wineries are mostly small and family-owned, many founded by Italian, Spanish and Basque immigrant families, and Tannat is promoted as Uruguay's signature.
