## Overview
Moldova is a small Eastern European country between Romania and Ukraine, with wine central to its economy and culture. It has a long viticultural history and large vineyard areas for its size.

## Typical grapes
Local varieties include Fetească Neagră, Fetească Albă, Fetească Regală and Rară Neagră (known in Romania as Băbească Neagră), which stand alongside international grapes such as Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc. Other varieties such as Saperavi, Pinot Noir, Aligoté and Muscat Ottonel are also grown.

## Wine styles
The country makes dry and off-dry reds and whites, rosé, sparkling wines and sweet wines. Fetească Neagră gives dark, spicy reds, often oaked, while Rară Neagră makes lighter, softer reds and is used in blends. Aromatic whites and bottle-fermented sparkling wines are important in the centre.

## What makes it special
Moldova has three Protected Geographical Indications for wine: Codru in the centre, Valul lui Traian in the south-west and Ștefan Vodă in the south-east. The continental climate brings cold winters and hot summers. Vast underground cellars in old limestone quarries are a national attraction: Cricova has over 100 km of tunnels, and Mileștii Mici holds a Guinness record for the world's largest wine collection.
