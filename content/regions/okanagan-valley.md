## Overview
The Okanagan Valley in British Columbia is British Columbia's largest wine region and Canada's second-largest after Ontario's Niagara Peninsula. It stretches about 200 km along a chain of lakes, with vineyards on the lake benches.

## Typical grapes
Merlot is the most planted red, with Pinot Noir, Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc and Syrah also important. Pinot Gris is the leading white, alongside Chardonnay and Riesling, among others.

## Wine styles
The warm south produces ripe Bordeaux-style reds and Syrah, while the cooler north suits Pinot Noir, aromatic whites and sparkling wine. Ice wine is also made, though less commonly than in Ontario.

## What makes it special
The valley has one of Canada's rare semi-arid zones, with hot, dry summers in the south near Osoyoos and long summer daylight at a northerly latitude. Lakes moderate temperatures, and irrigation is usually needed. Diverse glacial soils and a quality-focused VQA system under the BC Wine Authority define the region, with sub-GIs such as Golden Mile Bench (the first, in 2015) and Naramata Bench recognised.
