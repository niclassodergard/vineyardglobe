## Overview
Franciacorta is a hilly area in Lombardy south of Lake Iseo, near Brescia. Its DOCG is dedicated to sparkling wine made by the traditional method with secondary fermentation in bottle, and is widely regarded as Italy's most prestigious sparkling appellation.

## Typical grapes
Chardonnay dominates and gives structure and finesse. Pinot Noir (Pinot Nero) adds body and red fruit, and Pinot Blanc (Pinot Bianco) is permitted, adding softness. Erbamat, a local variety, may be used in small proportions. The separate Curtefranca DOC covers still wines from the same area.

## Wine styles
Styles include non-vintage Franciacorta, Satèn (a white-only, softer, lower-pressure style, always Brut), Rosé, and Millesimato from a single vintage. Minimum lees ageing is 18 months for non-vintage, 24 for Satèn and Rosé, 30 for Millesimato, and 60 for Riserva. Dosage ranges from Pas Dosé to Demi-Sec. Wines are creamy and fine, with brioche and citrus notes.

## What makes it special
Franciacorta became a DOCG in 1995, the first Italian wine made exclusively by bottle fermentation to gain that status. As with Champagne, the single word "Franciacorta" names the place, the method and the wine, and the term spumante is not used on labels. Glacial moraine soils, varied exposures and Lake Iseo's moderating effect, plus strict rules including hand harvesting, define the wines. Organic viticulture is widespread.
