## Overview
Provence is in south-east France along the Mediterranean coast. It is the world's leading region for dry rosé, which accounts for the large majority of its production.

## Typical grapes
Grenache, Cinsault and Syrah form the base of most rosés, with Mourvèdre as an important component. Mourvèdre also dominates Bandol reds. Rolle, known as Vermentino elsewhere, is the key white grape, and the local Tibouren adds character to some rosés. Côtes de Provence is by far the largest appellation.

## Wine styles
Provence rosé is pale, dry and delicate, with citrus, red fruit and herbal notes. Bandol produces structured, age-worthy reds, and Cassis makes aromatic whites. Whites and reds exist but are smaller in volume.

## What makes it special
The Mediterranean climate gives hot, dry summers, and the Mistral keeps the vines healthy. Limestone dominates in the west, around Mont Sainte-Victoire, while the east has crystalline schist and granite soils. The pale pink style is carefully made by direct pressing or short maceration, and the region has become fashionable worldwide. Bandol's amphitheatre-like slopes are especially prized.
