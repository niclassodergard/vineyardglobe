## Overview
Istria is the heart-shaped peninsula at the northern end of the Adriatic, most of which belongs to Croatia (smaller parts lie in Slovenia and Italy). Croatian Istria is one of the country's most prominent quality wine regions, with a long Venetian and Italian influence on its culture and cuisine.

## Typical grapes
Malvazija Istarska (Istrian Malvasia) is the signature white, planted more widely than any other grape. Teran is the main local red: deeply coloured and high in acidity. It belongs to the wider Refosco family but is a distinct variety from Friuli's Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso; across the border in Slovenia the same grape is called Refošk. Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon are also planted, as are Muscat and Chardonnay.

## Wine styles
Malvazija ranges from fresh, floral, citrus-and-almond whites to richer oaked or skin-contact styles that show texture and phenolic grip. Teran makes tart, peppery, dark-fruited reds with lively acidity. Sweet and sparkling wines are also made.

## What makes it special
Istria is often described through its soils: red terra rossa near the west coast, grey flysch of marl and sandstone inland, and pale limestone in the north-east, each shaping style. The Adriatic moderates the climate, and many small family producers work with local grapes. Truffles, olive oil and seafood are classic matches.
