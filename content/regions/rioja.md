## Overview
Rioja, in northern Spain along the Ebro river, was the country's first Denominación de Origen Calificada, awarded in 1991. It is Spain's best-known red wine region.

## Typical grapes
Tempranillo is the backbone of red Rioja. Garnacha (Grenache), Graciano and Mazuelo add colour, acidity and structure. Viura (Macabeo) is the principal white.

## Wine styles
Rioja is traditionally classified by ageing: young wines without an ageing category (often called Joven), Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva, each with minimum oak and bottle time. Traditional styles show American oak, with vanilla and coconut notes, and long ageing; modern wines use more French oak and fruit focus. White and rosado wines are also made.

## What makes it special
Three zones, Rioja Alta, Alavesa and Oriental, differ in altitude, soils and climate. The Sierra de Cantabria shelters the region from Atlantic weather. Longtime ageing in cellars, with release only when ready, is a hallmark. Producers have both traditional bodegas and single-vineyard innovators, and Viñedo Singular designations recognise individual sites.
