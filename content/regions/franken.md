## Overview
Franken, or Franconia, is a German Anbaugebiet in northern Bavaria, centred on the Main river and the city of Würzburg. It is known for dry white wines, especially Silvaner, and for the flat Bocksbeutel bottle.

## Typical grapes
Silvaner is the flagship grape, with Müller-Thurgau (also called Rivaner) also widely planted. Bacchus, an aromatic crossing, is another local favourite, and Riesling is grown on the warmest sites. Red grapes, led by Domina and Pinot Noir (Spätburgunder), make up a smaller share, with the best Pinot Noir from the red sandstone slopes of the far west around Bürgstadt and Klingenberg.

## Wine styles
Most wines are dry, with earthy, herbal, mineral Silvaner the signature, ranging from simple everyday wines to ageworthy Grosses Gewächs, the dry top-site wines of VDP member estates. Riesling, Müller-Thurgau and some red wines, mainly Pinot Noir, also appear.

## What makes it special
The continental climate brings cold winters and a risk of spring frost. Three districts have distinct soils: Mainviereck on Buntsandstein (red sandstone), Maindreieck on Muschelkalk (shell limestone) and Steigerwald on Keuper marls. The squat, flattened Bocksbeutel is protected under EU rules and reserved in Germany for Franken and a few neighbouring parts of Baden. The Würzburger Stein, a steep limestone vineyard overlooking Würzburg, is one of Germany's most famous sites.
