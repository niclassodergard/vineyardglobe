## Overview
The Bekaa Valley is a high plateau between the Mount Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon ranges, and it is the source of most of Lebanon's wine. Winemaking in the area is ancient, with Phoenician roots, and the Roman temple of Bacchus at Baalbek reflects wine's importance in antiquity. The modern industry dates from 1857, when Jesuit monks planted vines at Ksara near Zahlé.

## Typical grapes
Bordeaux and Rhône varieties dominate, including Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah and Merlot, along with the long-established Cinsault and Carignan. Indigenous whites such as Obaideh and Merwah exist, but international whites like Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc are more widespread.

## Wine styles
Reds are typically ripe and structured, often blended in a Bordeaux or Rhône manner, with dark fruit and spice. Chateau Musar, whose winery lies near the coast but whose red grapes come from the Bekaa, makes long-lived, distinctive Cabernet Sauvignon, Cinsault and Carignan blends. Dry whites and rosés are also made, and grapes are distilled into arak, the local anise spirit.

## What makes it special
Most vineyards sit at roughly 900 to 1,200 metres or higher, so cool nights offset hot, dry summers and help preserve acidity. Limestone and clay soils are common, and irrigation is sometimes needed. Winemaking has continued despite years of conflict, and the region is notable for its blend of ancient heritage and modern French-influenced practice.
