## Overview
Baden is Germany's southernmost and warmest wine region, stretching about 400 kilometres from Lake Constance in the south to Tauberfranken in the north, mostly along the east side of the Rhine valley opposite Alsace. It is Germany's third-largest region and the only one in EU wine zone B.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir (Spätburgunder) is the most planted variety, followed by Müller-Thurgau. Pinot Gris (Grauburgunder), Pinot Blanc (Weissburgunder) and Riesling thrive in different districts. Gutedel (Chasselas) is a specialty of Markgräflerland.

## Wine styles
Most wines are dry and fuller-bodied than elsewhere in Germany. Spätburgunder ranges from light and fruity to structured, oak-aged reds. Grauburgunder and Weissburgunder are rich and textured. The Prädikat system applies, and VDP member estates label dry wines from their top-classified sites (Grosse Lage) as Grosses Gewächs.

## What makes it special
Cooperatives handle a large share of production, though many private estates are noted for quality. The region is divided into nine districts (Bereiche), from Bodensee to Tauberfranken. Volcanic soils at the Kaiserstuhl store heat and suit Pinot varieties, while loess, limestone and granite appear in other districts. The warm climate and Rhine plain influences give ripeness that few other German regions match.
