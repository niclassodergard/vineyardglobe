## Overview
Sicily, the largest island in the Mediterranean, has a long winemaking history. It has shifted from bulk production toward distinctive, quality-focused wines.

## Typical grapes
Nero d'Avola is the leading red, giving dark-fruited, full-bodied wines. On Mount Etna, Nerello Mascalese and Nerello Cappuccio produce lighter, mineral reds. Grillo and Catarratto are key whites, Carricante is the grape of Etna Bianco, and Zibibbo (Muscat of Alexandria) is used on Pantelleria. Frappato is blended with Nero d'Avola in Cerasuolo di Vittoria, Sicily's only DOCG.

## Wine styles
Styles include ripe Nero d'Avola reds, elegant Etna Rosso and Etna Bianco, and fortified Marsala. Pantelleria produces sweet Passito wines from dried grapes.

## What makes it special
Etna's volcanic soils and high-altitude vineyards, some on old ungrafted vines, give wines often compared to Burgundy. Sunshine and sea breezes allow healthy ripening with little disease pressure. Marsala, created for export in the eighteenth century and traditionally based on Grillo, Catarratto and Inzolia, is made in various sweetness and ageing levels.
