## Overview
The Columbia Valley AVA, established in 1984, covers about a third of Washington State, with a small part extending into Oregon, and is home to the vast majority of Washington's vineyards. It lies east of the Cascade Mountains, which create a pronounced rain shadow.

## Typical grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot are the leading reds, with Syrah increasingly important. Chardonnay and Riesling lead the whites; Riesling built the state's early reputation.

## Wine styles
Reds are generally ripe and structured, with dark fruit and firm tannins yet fresh acidity from cool nights. Bordeaux-style blends and varietal Syrah are common. Riesling ranges from bone-dry to off-dry and sweet, and Chardonnay is made in both oaked and unoaked styles.

## What makes it special
Rainfall is very low, so irrigation, often from the Columbia and Yakima rivers, is essential. Summers are hot and sunny with large day-night temperature swings. Soils are mainly windblown loess and sand over gravels and silts left by the Missoula Floods at the end of the last Ice Age. Most vineyards are planted on their own roots, as phylloxera has struggled to spread in these sandy soils, though occasional hard winter freezes are a risk. Nested AVAs such as Yakima Valley, Walla Walla Valley, Red Mountain and Horse Heaven Hills show distinct styles, from the relatively cool upper Yakima Valley to the warm Wahluke Slope and Red Mountain.
