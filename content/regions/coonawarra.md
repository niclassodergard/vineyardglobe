## Overview
Coonawarra is a small Geographical Indication in South Australia's Limestone Coast zone, around the township of Coonawarra just north of Penola, roughly midway between Adelaide and Melbourne. Neighbouring GIs in the zone include Wrattonbully and Padthaway. It is one of Australia's most prized sites for Cabernet Sauvignon.

## Typical grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon dominates, giving the region its reputation. Shiraz (Syrah), Merlot and Chardonnay are also planted, with Cabernet often blended with Merlot.

## Wine styles
Coonawarra Cabernet is typically medium- to full-bodied with blackcurrant, mint and eucalyptus notes, firm but fine tannins and good ageing potential. Shiraz here is peppery and cooler in style than from warmer Australian regions. Chardonnay is a minor but notable white.

## What makes it special
The famous terra rossa is a thin layer of red loam over limestone, with a high water table beneath. It forms a narrow, cigar-shaped strip only a kilometre or two wide, flanked by darker, less prized soils. The GI boundary was fiercely disputed, and the final border, settled in the early 2000s, includes land beyond the red strip. The climate is cool, with a long ripening season moderated by proximity to the Southern Ocean. John Riddoch founded the Penola Fruit Colony in 1890 and planted the first vines soon after; his stone winery is now part of Wynns Coonawarra Estate.
