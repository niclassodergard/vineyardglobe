## Overview
Yamanashi Prefecture, west of Tokyo and in the shadow of Mount Fuji, is Japan's historic centre of wine. It has more wineries than any other prefecture, most of them in the Kofu Basin, and in 2013 Yamanashi became Japan's first wine Geographical Indication.

## Typical grapes
Koshu is the signature white, a pink-skinned variety of largely Vitis vinifera ancestry, recognised by the OIV for wine labels in 2010. Muscat Bailey A is a red hybrid of Bailey and Muscat Hamburg, bred in Japan by Zenbei Kawakami from a 1927 crossing and registered with the OIV in 2013. Merlot, Chardonnay and Cabernet Sauvignon are also grown.

## Wine styles
Koshu is usually dry, with delicate citrus, pear and mineral notes, fresh acidity and subtle aroma, and is often made sur lie for texture; it suits Japanese cuisine. Sparkling and orange-hued skin-contact Koshu are also made. Muscat Bailey A produces light, fruity reds with strawberry and candy notes.

## What makes it special
Rain and humidity during the growing season, plus typhoon risk, are major challenges. Many growers use overhead pergola training (tana), which raises fruit above the humid ground and aids airflow. Koshu is thought to have grown here for centuries, and the Katsunuma district, now part of Koshu city and home to many family wineries, is its heartland. Quality-minded producers have raised Koshu's profile abroad.
