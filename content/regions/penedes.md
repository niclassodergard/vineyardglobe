## Overview
Penedès lies in Catalonia, between Barcelona and Tarragona, running inland from the Mediterranean coast. It is the heartland of Cava, Spain's traditional-method sparkling wine, and also has its own DO Penedès, mainly for still wines. The town of Sant Sadurní d'Anoia is the centre of Cava production.

## Typical grapes
Cava is based on the native trio of Macabeo (Viura), Xarel·lo and Parellada. Xarel·lo gives body and earthiness, Macabeo freshness and Parellada delicacy. Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are also permitted, and rosé can use red grapes such as Grenache (Garnacha), Monastrell and Trepat. Still wines use these grapes plus international and Spanish reds.

## Wine styles
Cava is made by the traditional method, with the second fermentation in bottle. Basic Cava needs at least 9 months on the lees; the premium Cava de Guarda Superior tier covers Reserva (at least 18 months), Gran Reserva (at least 30 months) and Cava de Paraje Calificado (at least 36 months, from a single site). Sweetness ranges from Brut Nature to Dulce. From the 1960s, producers such as Torres and Jean León pioneered international varieties in Spanish still wine here.

## What makes it special
Altitude divides the region into the coastal Baix Penedès, the middle Penedès Central and the higher Alt Penedès, giving a range of ripeness and acidity. The Cava DO covers several parts of Spain, but the great majority is produced in Catalonia, mostly in Penedès. Some leading producers have left the Cava DO to follow stricter rules: the Corpinnat group, formed in 2018, requires organic, hand-harvested grapes from its defined area, and DO Penedès has its own organic sparkling category, Clàssic Penedès.
