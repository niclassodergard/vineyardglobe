## Overview
The Mosel in western Germany follows a winding river and its tributaries, the Saar and Ruwer. It is among the world's most celebrated sources of Riesling.

## Typical grapes
Riesling dominates the steep slopes. Müller-Thurgau and Elbling are also grown, but in the great sites Riesling is almost exclusive.

## Wine styles
Wines are typically light in body and alcohol, with high acidity, and flavours of citrus, green apple and peach. Many are made off-dry, balancing sugar against acidity. Germany's Prädikat system (Kabinett, Spätlese, Auslese and above) classifies wines by grape ripeness at harvest, not by final sweetness. Dry (trocken) wines are increasingly important, including the VDP's Grosses Gewächs from top sites.

## What makes it special
Extremely steep vineyards, among the steepest in the world, face the river to capture sunlight and its reflected warmth. Blue and grey Devonian slate soils store heat and give a distinctive mineral character. Famous sites include the Wehlener Sonnenuhr, Bernkasteler Doctor and the Saar's Scharzhofberg. Wines age very well thanks to high acidity.
