## Overview
Santorini is a volcanic island in the Aegean Sea, shaped by a huge eruption roughly 3,600 years ago. Its winemaking tradition stretches back thousands of years.

## Typical grapes
Assyrtiko is the island's flagship white, partnered by Athiri and Aidani. The reds Mandilaria and Mavrotragano are grown in small quantities. Assyrtiko holds its high acidity even in a hot climate.

## Wine styles
Dry Assyrtiko is full of citrus, saline minerality and bracing acidity, with some producers using oak or lees ageing. Vinsanto is a sweet wine made from sun-dried grapes and aged in barrel, rich with dried fruit and caramel notes.

## What makes it special
Soils are volcanic ash, pumice and lava with very little organic matter, and phylloxera has not taken hold, so many vines are ungrafted and very old. Vines are trained into low basket shapes (kouloura) that protect grapes from strong winds and sun. Rainfall is extremely low, and the vines draw moisture from sea mist and humidity at night.
