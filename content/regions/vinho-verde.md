## Overview
Vinho Verde is one of Portugal's largest demarcated regions, covering the green, rainy northwest between the Minho river and the Douro around Porto. It was demarcated in 1908. "Verde" refers to youth and freshness, not colour. It is the country's best-known source of light, inexpensive white wine.

## Typical grapes
Loureiro gives floral, citrus aromas, while Arinto (locally Pedernã) and Trajadura (Treixadura) add acidity and body. Alvarinho (Albariño) is the star of Monção e Melgaço, making fuller, more age-worthy wines. Avesso is prominent inland in Baião and Amarante. Vinhão is the main red, dark and tannic.

## Wine styles
Most wines are white, low in alcohol, high in acidity and often released young, sometimes with a light spritz from added or retained carbon dioxide. Single-varietal Alvarinho and Loureiro, rosé, sparkling and some red wines are also produced. Reds are traditionally very deep coloured and sharp.

## What makes it special
The Atlantic influence brings high rainfall and moderate temperatures, and soils are mostly granitic. Traditionally vines were trained high, up trees (vinha de enforcado) and on pergolas (ramadas), to leave ground free for other crops, and some growers still use pergolas, though modern vineyards mostly use wire trellises. Nine sub-regions differ in grape and style, with Monção e Melgaço facing Galicia's Rías Baixas across the Minho.
