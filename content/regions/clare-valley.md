## Overview
The Clare Valley is a Geographical Indication in South Australia, about 120 km north of Adelaide. Despite its name it is a series of narrow valleys and ridges running north to south, at roughly 300 to 500 metres, and it is famous for dry Riesling.

## Typical grapes
Riesling is the signature white. Shiraz (Syrah) and Cabernet Sauvignon are the leading reds, and Semillon and Chardonnay are made in smaller quantities.

## Wine styles
Clare Riesling is dry, with lime and citrus when young and toast and kerosene notes with bottle age. It is typically bottled under screwcap. Shiraz is rich and peppery and Cabernet is firm, with ripe dark fruit and good structure.

## What makes it special
Although the region is warm in the day, elevation and the influence of cool breezes bring cold nights that preserve acidity. Soils vary by sub-region. Watervale has red-brown loam over limestone, giving more floral, delicate Riesling, while Polish Hill River has slate and shale, producing tighter, more mineral wines. In 2000 a group of Clare winemakers jointly bottled their Rieslings under screwcap, a landmark in Australia's move away from cork. Vines were first planted in the 1840s, and Jesuits founded Sevenhill Cellars in 1851, one of the region's oldest wineries.
