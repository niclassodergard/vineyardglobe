## Overview
Stellenbosch, east of Cape Town in the Western Cape, is South Africa's best-known wine district and the centre of its wine industry. Founded in 1679, it is also home to the country's main wine research and viticulture university.

## Typical grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon is the most prestigious variety, often blended with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot. Syrah (Shiraz) and Pinotage, South Africa's own cross of Pinot Noir and Cinsault, are also important. Chenin Blanc and Chardonnay lead the whites.

## Wine styles
Cabernet and Bordeaux-style blends are structured, with dark fruit and firm tannin. Cape blends usually include a significant share of Pinotage. Pinotage itself ranges from fruity to serious. Chenin Blanc, often from old bush vines, can be fresh or richer and oaked, and there are also good Chardonnays.

## What makes it special
The mountainous terrain, with granite, sandstone and shale soils, offers a range of mesoclimates, and cooling breezes from False Bay moderate the Mediterranean heat. Wards under the Wine of Origin system, like Simonsberg-Stellenbosch and Jonkershoek Valley, show distinct styles, and the district has a long, influential winemaking history.
