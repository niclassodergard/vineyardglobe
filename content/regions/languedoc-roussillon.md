## Overview
Languedoc-Roussillon stretches along France's Mediterranean coast from Nîmes to the Spanish border. It is the country's largest wine-producing area by volume and has transformed from bulk wine into a source of quality and value.

## Typical grapes
Grenache, Syrah, Carignan and Mourvèdre make up most red blends, with old-vine Carignan highly valued. Cinsault is common in rosé. Whites include Chardonnay, Grenache Blanc, Picpoul for Picpoul de Pinet and Mauzac for Blanquette de Limoux.

## Wine styles
Reds are generous, spicy and herbal, often blended. Limoux produces sparkling wine, claimed as among the oldest sparkling traditions. Roussillon is known for vins doux naturels such as Banyuls and Maury, along with Rivesaltes.

## What makes it special
The region has great geological diversity, including schist, limestone and basalt. The Languedoc AOC now has a hierarchy of crus, some (such as Pic Saint-Loup and La Clape) promoted to their own appellations, and Pays d'Oc IGP allows creative winemaking outside the appellation rules. Garrigue scrub, sunshine and old bush vines give distinctive character, and prices remain accessible.
