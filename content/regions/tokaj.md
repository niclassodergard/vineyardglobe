## Overview
Tokaj is a volcanic region in northeast Hungary near the Slovakian border, with a long reputation for sweet wines. Its vineyards were among the first in Europe to be classified, in the eighteenth century.

## Typical grapes
Furmint is the main grape, prized for high acidity and its susceptibility to noble rot. Hárslevelű adds floral, spicy character, and Sárga Muskotály (Muscat Blanc) is used in smaller amounts.

## Wine styles
Tokaji Aszú is made by adding botrytised, shrivelled berries (aszú) to base wine or must, with sweetness traditionally measured in puttonyos. Current rules require at least 120 g/l of residual sugar, and stating puttonyos on the label is now optional. Eszencia is an extremely concentrated sweet nectar. Dry Furmint has grown in popularity, giving mineral, structured whites with strong acidity.

## What makes it special
Volcanic soils, rhyolite and clay, combine with autumn mists from the Bodrog and Tisza rivers that encourage noble rot. Wines age in cellars carved into volcanic tuff, which are covered with distinctive cellar mould. The region is a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape.
