## Overview
Central Otago, in the southern South Island, is one of the world's southernmost wine regions and New Zealand's highest, with most vineyards at roughly 200 to 450 metres. Its landscape of schist mountains and arid valleys is dramatic, and the region is overwhelmingly known for Pinot Noir.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir accounts for the majority of plantings. Pinot Gris, Riesling and Chardonnay are the key whites, and they are made in smaller volumes with fresh acidity.

## Wine styles
Pinot Noir is typically ripe and concentrated, with dark cherry, thyme and spice character, and more body than many cool-climate examples. Whites are crisp and aromatic, with Riesling and Pinot Gris being the most notable.

## What makes it special
This is New Zealand's only truly continental region, with hot, dry days, cold nights, low rainfall and a high frost risk. Soils are mostly schist-derived gravels and silts, with windblown loess in places. Sub-regions such as Bannockburn, Gibbston and Bendigo each show distinct styles, and the region's remoteness, intense sunlight and short growing season give wines of clear character.
