## Overview
The Douro Valley in northern Portugal follows the Douro River inland from the Atlantic. It is among the world's oldest demarcated wine regions, with demarcation begun in 1756, and is the home of Port.

## Typical grapes
Touriga Nacional gives structure, colour and floral aromatics, while Touriga Franca adds perfume and balance. Tinta Roriz (Tempranillo) and Tinta Barroca are also widely planted, usually in field blends of many varieties. White Port and dry whites use grapes such as Viosinho and Rabigato.

## Wine styles
Port is a red wine fortified with grape spirit during fermentation, which leaves residual sugar. Styles include Ruby, Tawny, Late Bottled Vintage and Vintage Port. Since the late twentieth century, dry Douro DOC reds and whites have grown in importance.

## What makes it special
Vines grow on steep schist terraces carved into the valley slopes, often harvested by hand. Summers are hot and dry, and rainfall decreases moving upriver from Baixo Corgo to Douro Superior. Traditionally the grapes were trodden in stone lagares. The region is a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape.
