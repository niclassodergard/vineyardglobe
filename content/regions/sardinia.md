## Overview
Sardinia is Italy's second-largest island, in the western Mediterranean. Its isolation, strong sunshine and sea winds have preserved distinct grape varieties and traditions. It has one DOCG, Vermentino di Gallura, along with many DOCs, and the island-wide Isola dei Nuraghi IGT.

## Typical grapes
Cannonau, the local name for Grenache, is the most planted red, made into robust wines. Carignano (Carignan) thrives on the sandy soils of the southwest. Vermentino is the leading white, and local varieties such as Vernaccia di Oristano, Monica, Bovale and Nuragus are also important.

## Wine styles
Cannonau is rich, warm and spicy with ripe red fruit. Carignano del Sulcis is deep, round and fruity. Vermentino di Gallura is fresh, aromatic and saline. Vernaccia di Oristano is a sherry-like wine aged in partly filled casks under a film of flor yeast. Rosé and sweet wines also appear.

## What makes it special
Granite soils in Gallura, sandy soils in Sulcis, wind, and old bush vines shape the wines; some Sulcis vines on sand are ungrafted. The mountainous Nuoro province, a heartland of Cannonau, is known as one of the world's "Blue Zones" of exceptional longevity, and local red wine often features in that story, though no direct health link has been proven. Centuries of Aragonese and Spanish rule are often credited with bringing Cannonau and Carignano to the island, although some Sardinians argue that Cannonau is native.
