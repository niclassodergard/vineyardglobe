## Overview
Piedmont lies in northwest Italy at the foot of the Alps, and its name means "foot of the mountain". The Langhe and Monferrato hills produce some of Italy's most prestigious wines.

## Typical grapes
Nebbiolo makes Barolo and Barbaresco, both DOCG wines. Barbera gives deeply coloured, high-acid reds, and Dolcetto is a softer everyday red. Moscato Bianco (Muscat Blanc) is used for Moscato d'Asti and Asti, while Cortese makes the dry whites of Gavi.

## Wine styles
Barolo and Barbaresco are pale in colour but high in tannin and acidity, with aromas of rose, tar and cherry; they age for decades. Barbera d'Asti and Barbera d'Alba are fruitier and more approachable. Moscato d'Asti is lightly sparkling, low in alcohol and sweet.

## What makes it special
Autumn fog (nebbia) is often cited as the origin of Nebbiolo's name. Barolo soils differ between the younger Tortonian marls of La Morra and Barolo, giving more perfumed wines, and the older Serravallian soils of Serralunga and Monforte, with more sandstone, giving more structured wines. Winemaking debates between traditionalists (long maceration, large botti) and modernists (rotary fermenters, barriques) shaped the region.
