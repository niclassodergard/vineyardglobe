## Overview
Champagne, in north-eastern France around Reims and Épernay, is the home of the world's most celebrated sparkling wine. Its cool climate produces high-acid base wines ideal for sparkling production.

## Typical grapes
Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier are the three main varieties. Chardonnay brings finesse and citrus, Pinot Noir body and structure, and Meunier fruitiness and early appeal.

## Wine styles
Non-vintage brut is the house style; vintage, rosé, Blanc de Blancs and Blanc de Noirs are also made. Prestige cuvées such as Dom Pérignon or Cristal are aged longer. Sweetness ranges from Brut Nature to Doux.

## What makes it special
Wines are made by the traditional method, with secondary fermentation in bottle and lees ageing; non-vintage wines need at least 15 months total and vintage wines at least 3 years. Chalk subsoils, especially in the Marne and Côte des Blancs, drain well yet retain water for dry spells; the Côte des Bar lies on Kimmeridgian marl. Houses buy grapes from many growers, while grower-producers have grown in prominence. Reserve wines from earlier vintages help maintain a consistent house style. The traditional échelle des crus rates whole villages, 17 of them Grand Cru and around 40 Premier Cru.
