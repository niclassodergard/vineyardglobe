## Overview
Armenia's best-known wine area is Vayots Dzor, around the village of Areni in the south of the country. Nearby, the Areni-1 cave held a winemaking installation, with a press and fermentation vat, radiocarbon-dated to about 4100 BC, roughly 6,100 years ago. It is the oldest known winery found so far. Other growing areas include the Ararat Valley, Armavir, Aragatsotn and Tavush.

## Typical grapes
Areni (Areni Noir) is the most important native red, late-ripening and usually made as a varietal wine. Voskehat, whose name means "golden berry", is the key native white. Other local grapes such as Kangun, Garan Dmak and Haghtanak are also planted, alongside international varieties. Other local and international varieties are also planted.

## Wine styles
Areni gives fresh, red-fruited, spicy reds with bright acidity, often showing a savoury edge. Voskehat makes aromatic, structured whites. Some producers have revived traditional clay amphorae called karas (plural karasner), the Armenian counterpart of Georgian qvevri, for fermentation and ageing.

## What makes it special
Vineyards in Vayots Dzor sit at roughly 1,000 to 1,800 metres, with hot, dry summers, cold winters and big day-night temperature swings, and many old vines are grown on their own roots. The combination of an ancient winemaking record, native varieties and traditional clay-vessel methods makes Armenia a fascinating study in the origins of wine.
