## Overview
The Hunter Valley, about two hours north of Sydney in New South Wales, is one of Australia's oldest wine regions, with plantings dating to the 1820s. Despite a hot, humid climate, it produces wines with real distinction. The Lower Hunter around Pokolbin is the heartland, while the Upper Hunter lies further inland.

## Typical grapes
Sémillon is the signature white, and Syrah (Shiraz in Australia) is the main red. Chardonnay is widely planted and is a commercial mainstay, while other varieties are grown in smaller amounts.

## Wine styles
Hunter Sémillon is picked early and made unoaked, giving low-alcohol, lemony wines, often around 10-11.5% abv. With bottle age they develop toasty, honeyed complexity and are among the world's most distinctive white wines. Shiraz is medium-bodied, earthy and savoury, quite different from South Australian styles.

## What makes it special
Rain during the growing season and around harvest is a persistent challenge, so growers pick early and manage canopies carefully. The unusual ageing profile of Sémillon, from a very light young wine into a rich, complex one without oak, is the region's calling card, as is its proximity to Sydney and its strong wine-tourism culture.
