## Overview
Casablanca Valley lies between Santiago and Valparaíso, close to the Pacific coast. Planting began in earnest in the 1980s, and it became Chile's pioneering cool-climate white-wine region.

## Typical grapes
Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay dominate, with Pinot Noir increasingly important. Syrah, Merlot and others are planted in warmer pockets, and other whites include Riesling and Gewürztraminer in smaller amounts.

## Wine styles
Sauvignon Blanc is crisp and zesty, with citrus and herbal notes. Chardonnay ranges from fresh, unoaked to richer styles, while Pinot Noir is light to medium-bodied with red fruit. Cool-climate Syrah is peppery and elegant.

## What makes it special
Morning fog and cool Pacific breezes moderate temperatures, giving a long growing season in a country otherwise known for warmth. This coastal influence contrasts with the hot interior valleys. Spring frost is a risk, and harvest is often later than in other Chilean regions. The Casablanca model led to wider exploration of Chile's coastal zones, such as San Antonio and Leyda.
