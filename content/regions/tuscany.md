## Overview
Tuscany in central Italy is home to some of the country's most famous red wines. Its hills around Florence and Siena are dominated by Sangiovese.

## Typical grapes
Sangiovese is the backbone of Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino (Brunello is the local name for Sangiovese) and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano (where it is called Prugnolo Gentile). Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Cabernet Franc feature in the Bolgheri coast wines. Vernaccia is the main white grape of San Gimignano.

## Wine styles
Sangiovese wines are typically medium-bodied with high acidity, firm tannins and flavours of sour cherry and dried herbs. Brunello is fuller and long-aged. Super Tuscans, such as those from Bolgheri, emerged outside traditional rules, often with Bordeaux varieties.

## What makes it special
Chianti Classico is distinguished by its black rooster emblem and Gran Selezione top tier. Brunello di Montalcino cannot be released until five years after the harvest (six for Riserva), including at least two years in oak. Soils include galestro, a crumbly schistous marl, and alberese limestone, and the Mediterranean climate is moderated by altitude and the sea near Bolgheri.
