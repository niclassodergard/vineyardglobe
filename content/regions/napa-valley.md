## Overview
Napa Valley is a narrow valley north of San Francisco Bay in California, and the most famous American fine-wine region. It is a single large AVA (1981) containing more than a dozen nested sub-AVAs.

## Typical grapes
Cabernet Sauvignon dominates, giving dark, concentrated, structured reds. Merlot and Cabernet Franc feature in Bordeaux-style blends. Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc are the main whites.

## Wine styles
Full-bodied, ripe Cabernet Sauvignon with blackcurrant fruit, firm tannins and generous oak is the benchmark. Many top wines are blends. Whites include rich Chardonnay and fresher Sauvignon Blanc, while Carneros also suits sparkling wine.

## What makes it special
Warm, dry summers with cool nights, moderated by fog and breezes from San Pablo Bay, give reliable ripeness. Napa has an unusually diverse mix of volcanic and sedimentary soils, so valley-floor, bench and mountain sites differ markedly. The 1976 Judgment of Paris tasting put it on the world map, and its prestige and high land values support a strong tradition of premium, often cult-status, wines.
