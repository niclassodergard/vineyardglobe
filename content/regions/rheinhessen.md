## Overview
Rheinhessen is Germany's largest wine region, lying in the bend of the Rhine between Mainz, Worms and Bingen. Long associated with mass-market Liebfraumilch, a name that originated around Worms, it has reinvented itself as one of the country's most dynamic quality regions, driven by a young generation of growers.

## Typical grapes
Riesling is the leading quality grape, especially on the best slopes. Müller-Thurgau and Silvaner (Sylvaner) are widely planted, with Silvaner enjoying a revival as a serious, mineral dry white. Pinot Blanc (Weissburgunder) and Pinot Gris (Grauburgunder) are important, and Pinot Noir (Spätburgunder) leads among quality reds, alongside the widely planted Dornfelder.

## Wine styles
Dry (trocken) wines now dominate. Riesling ranges from fresh, everyday wines to powerful, age-worthy dry wines labelled Grosses Gewächs (GG) from top sites classified by the VDP growers' association. Silvaner is herbal and textured, while the Pinots produce structured, food-friendly whites and increasingly elegant reds.

## What makes it special
Soil diversity is remarkable: loess and loam, limestone, and the red slate and sandstone of the Rotliegend at the Roter Hang between Nierstein and Nackenheim, which yields rich, mineral Rieslings. The Rhine-facing Rheinterrasse holds many top vineyards, while the rolling hills inland give the region its nickname, the land of a thousand hills. Surrounding uplands keep it relatively warm and dry. The region is divided into three districts (Bereiche): Bingen, Nierstein and Wonnegau, the last home to several of Germany's most celebrated growers.
