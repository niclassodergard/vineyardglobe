## Overview
Texas Hill Country is an AVA in central Texas, west of Austin and north of San Antonio, established in 1991. Covering about 15,000 square miles, it is one of the largest AVAs in the United States, usually ranked third after the Upper Mississippi River Valley and Ohio River Valley. It is a major centre of Texas wine tourism, with many wineries.

## Typical grapes
Heat-tolerant varieties do best. Tempranillo has become a signature red, alongside Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah. Tannat, Mourvèdre and Sangiovese are also popular. Whites include Viognier, Chardonnay, Roussanne and Vermentino. Many Hill Country wines are made partly from grapes grown in the Texas High Plains.

## Wine styles
Styles range from Spanish- and Rhône-inspired reds and blends to aromatic whites and rosé. Wines tend to be ripe and full-flavoured, reflecting the warm climate.

## What makes it special
Heat, humidity, spring frost and disease pressure make viticulture challenging, so growers favour late-budding, heat-tolerant varieties. Limestone and granite soils and rolling hills give some relief. Within the AVA, in Gillespie County, sit two smaller AVAs that predate it: Bell Mountain (1986), the first AVA in Texas, and Fredericksburg in the Texas Hill Country (1988).
