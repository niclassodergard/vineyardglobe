## Overview
The Rhône Valley follows the river south from Vienne to Avignon and divides into two very different halves, the Northern and Southern Rhône.

## Typical grapes
In the north, Syrah makes the reds, sometimes co-fermented with a little Viognier in Côte-Rôtie. Viognier is the white of Condrieu, while Marsanne and Roussanne make white Hermitage. In the south, Grenache (Garnacha elsewhere) leads blends with Syrah and Mourvèdre.

## Wine styles
Northern reds are peppery, dark and structured, from steep granite slopes. Southern wines are warmer and fuller, with spice and garrigue notes. Côtes du Rhône is the large regional appellation, and rosé is made at Tavel.

## What makes it special
Châteauneuf-du-Pape allows up to 18 grape varieties and is known for its galets roulés, large stones that retain heat. Côte-Rôtie's terraced vineyards are famously steep. The Mistral wind keeps the vineyards dry and healthy. Quality tiers run from Côtes du Rhône to Côtes du Rhône Villages, Villages with a named village, and finally the crus, which include all the northern appellations and southern names such as Châteauneuf-du-Pape and Gigondas.
