## Overview
Cyprus is an island in the eastern Mediterranean with one of the oldest winemaking traditions in the world. Most quality vineyards lie on the slopes of the Troodos Mountains, where altitude tempers the heat.

## Typical grapes
Xynisteri is the most widely planted white grape on the island and makes fresh, dry whites. Mavro is the most planted red, and Maratheftiko is a deeply coloured indigenous red that many producers see as the island's most promising quality variety, though it is tricky to grow because its flowers often need help with pollination. International varieties such as Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah are also grown. Mavro and Xynisteri are also the grapes of Commandaria.

## Wine styles
Commandaria is a sweet wine made from sun-dried Xynisteri and Mavro grown in 14 designated villages on the southern Troodos foothills north of Limassol. It must be aged in oak for at least two years. Its name comes from the Grande Commanderie of the crusading Knights Hospitaller in the Middle Ages, and it is often claimed to be the oldest named wine still in production. Dry whites, rosés and reds are also made.

## What makes it special
Cyprus has PDO zones for Commandaria, Pitsilia, Laona Akamas, Vouni Panagias-Ampelitis and Krasochoria Lemesou (with sub-zones Afames and Laona), plus PGIs. Phylloxera never reached the island, so many vines are ungrafted, and old, low-yielding bush vines are common on the high slopes. Indigenous varieties are the focus of a modern quality revival.
