## Overview
Etna DOC covers the slopes of Mount Etna, Europe's tallest active volcano, in eastern Sicily. It was Sicily's first DOC, recognised in 1968. Vineyards form a crescent around the north, east and south of the mountain. The producers' consortium has applied for DOCG status, which was still pending at the time of writing.

## Typical grapes
Nerello Mascalese is the main red, with Nerello Cappuccio (Mantellato) as a minor blending partner. Carricante is the key white, high in acidity, with Catarratto permitted in smaller amounts in Etna Bianco. Minella Bianca is also found in small quantities.

## Wine styles
Etna Rosso is pale to medium in colour, with high acidity, fine tannins and red fruit, spice and smoky mineral notes, often compared with Pinot Noir or Nebbiolo. Etna Bianco is lean, saline and citrusy, and the best age well. Rosato and traditional-method sparkling wines are also made.

## What makes it special
Volcanic soils of lava and ash, vineyards climbing to around 1,000 metres, large day and night temperature swings, and old, often ungrafted bush vines on terraces give distinctive wines. Etna's lava flows have shaped the vineyards, and individually named sites called contrade (well over a hundred are officially recognised) are increasingly used on labels. Etna Bianco Superiore, with at least 80 percent Carricante, may come only from the commune of Milo on the eastern slopes.
