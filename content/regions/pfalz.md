## Overview
The Pfalz is Germany's second-largest wine region, lying along the Haardt hills west of the Rhine near the French border of Alsace. It is one of the country's warmest and driest regions.

## Typical grapes
Riesling is the leading variety, with Dornfelder and Müller-Thurgau also widely planted. Pinot Blanc (Weissburgunder), Pinot Gris (Grauburgunder) and Pinot Noir (Spätburgunder) have grown significantly in quality.

## Wine styles
The Pfalz is known for dry, full-bodied Riesling with ripe stone-fruit flavours, especially in the Mittelhaardt villages of Forst, Deidesheim and Wachenheim. Dry Pinot whites are rich and textured, and Pinot Noir has gained a strong reputation for quality.

## What makes it special
The Haardt mountains (the northern extension of the Vosges) shelter the vineyards from rain, giving warm, sunny conditions. Soils are varied, including sandstone, limestone, basalt and loess. The Grosses Gewächs designation, by the VDP growers' association, marks top dry wines from classified sites. The Deutsche Weinstraße (German Wine Route), created in 1935, runs the length of the region.
