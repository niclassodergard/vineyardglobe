## Overview
England has become one of the world's most exciting cool-climate sparkling wine regions. Vineyards are concentrated in the south-east, especially Sussex, Kent and Hampshire, with growth also in Surrey, Essex and the south-west. Quality wine can be labelled under the PDO "English Wine", and in 2022 Sussex became the first English county with its own PDO, for both still and sparkling wine.

## Typical grapes
The classic Champagne trio dominates plantings: Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Meunier (Pinot Meunier). These ripen in a cool climate with high acidity ideal for sparkling wine. Among still wines, aromatic Bacchus, a German cross, is a signature white, alongside Pinot Blanc, Seyval Blanc and Ortega.

## Wine styles
Most prestigious are bottle-fermented sparkling wines made by the traditional method, including Blanc de Blancs, Blanc de Noirs and rosé, often given long lees ageing. Still Bacchus has grassy, elderflower and citrus notes reminiscent of Sauvignon Blanc, and still Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are increasingly made in warm years.

## What makes it special
Chalk ridges such as the South Downs in Sussex and Hampshire and the North Downs in Kent belong to the same Cretaceous chalk formation that underlies Champagne across the Channel, while other vineyards lie on clay, sand and greensand. Warmer summers have improved ripeness, though frost, rain at flowering and vintage variation remain challenges. Producers have won international acclaim, and Champagne houses such as Taittinger and Pommery have invested in English vineyards.
