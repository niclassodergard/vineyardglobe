## Overview
Kakheti, in eastern Georgia, produces most of the country's wine and sits at the foothills of the Greater Caucasus. Georgia is often cited as a cradle of winemaking, with archaeological evidence of winemaking around 8,000 years ago.

## Typical grapes
Rkatsiteli is the main white grape, and Saperavi, a rare teinturier with red flesh, is the main red. Mtsvane is another white used for blends and amber wines, including the classic dry Tsinandali blend with Rkatsiteli, and Kisi and Khikhvi are other prized local whites.

## Wine styles
Traditional amber wines are made by fermenting white grapes with skins, seeds and sometimes stems in buried clay vessels called qvevri, giving tannic, nutty, dried-apricot character. Saperavi produces deep, structured reds, and appellations such as Mukuzani and Kindzmarauli are known for dry and naturally semi-sweet reds respectively. European-style wines are also made.

## What makes it special
Qvevri winemaking is recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage. Winters are cold, summers hot, and the Alazani Valley provides fertile alluvial soils. Georgia has hundreds of indigenous grape varieties.
