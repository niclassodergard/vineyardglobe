## Overview
Burgenland is Austria's easternmost wine state, on the Hungarian border and centred on the shallow Lake Neusiedl. It is Austria's main red-wine area and home to some of the world's finest sweet wines.

## Typical grapes
Blaufränkisch is the leading red, with Zweigelt and St. Laurent also important, often in blends. Welschriesling and Chardonnay are key whites, alongside Pinot Blanc (Weissburgunder) and Muscat varieties for sweet wines.

## Wine styles
Blaufränkisch is peppery, with dark cherry and fresh acidity, ageing well. Zweigelt is softer and fruitier. Around the lake, noble rot is encouraged and produces Beerenauslese, Trockenbeerenauslese and Ausbruch. Burgenland's DAC appellations are Neusiedlersee (Zweigelt-based reds and sweet wines), Leithaberg, Rosalia, Mittelburgenland and Eisenberg (both Blaufränkisch) and Ruster Ausbruch, each with its own rules.

## What makes it special
Lake Neusiedl's shallow waters create misty autumn mornings that promote botrytis, while the warm Pannonian climate ripens reds. The Seewinkel, on the flat eastern shore, is the heart of sweet-wine production, and Mittelburgenland is so devoted to its leading red that it is nicknamed Blaufränkischland. Leithaberg's limestone and schist hills give elegant, mineral wines, and Eisenberg's iron-rich slate and loam soils give spicy, mineral Blaufränkisch. The small free city (Freistadt) of Rust has been famed for its Ausbruch since at least the 17th century.
