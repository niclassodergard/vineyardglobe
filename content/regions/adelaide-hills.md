## Overview
The Adelaide Hills is a Geographical Indication in the Mount Lofty Ranges, just east of Adelaide in South Australia. Its Geographical Indication was registered in 1998, and with many vineyards above 400 metres it is one of the state's highest and coolest wine regions.

## Typical grapes
Sauvignon Blanc is the most widely grown variety, making zesty, tropical-to-citrus wines. Chardonnay and Pinot Noir follow, and both are used for still and sparkling wines. Shiraz from warmer, lower sites is peppery and medium-bodied, and Pinot Gris, Grüner Veltliner and other varieties are also planted.

## Wine styles
Crisp, aromatic Sauvignon Blanc, elegant Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are the stars, and the region supplies fruit for traditional-method sparkling wines. Cooler sites make restrained, fresh wines with good acidity.

## What makes it special
Altitude brings cool conditions and relatively high rainfall, though spring frost can be a hazard. Two official sub-regions, Lenswood and Piccadilly Valley, are known for fine Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. The region stretches about 70 km along the ranges, so conditions vary from cool, high, wetter sites in the centre to warmer, drier slopes in the north and south. Its closeness to Adelaide has made it a leading cool-climate source for South Australia.
