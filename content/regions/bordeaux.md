## Overview
Bordeaux, in south-west France, is centred on the Gironde estuary and its tributaries, the Garonne and Dordogne. It is among the world's most famous fine-wine regions and is largely built around the château system.

## Typical grapes
On the Left Bank, Cabernet Sauvignon leads blends, giving structure and blackcurrant fruit. On the Right Bank, Merlot dominates, with Cabernet Franc adding perfume. Petit Verdot and Malbec play minor roles. Whites rely on Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon.

## Wine styles
Left Bank reds (Médoc, Graves) are firm, tannic and age-worthy; Right Bank reds (Saint-Émilion, Pomerol) are plusher and rounder. Dry whites come from Pessac-Léognan and Entre-Deux-Mers, while Sauternes and Barsac produce luscious botrytised sweet wines.

## What makes it special
Gravel soils on the Left Bank and clay and limestone on the Right Bank suit different grapes, and the maritime climate brings vintage variation. The 1855 Classification ranked Médoc châteaux (plus Haut-Brion from Graves) into five growths, with a separate ranking for Sauternes and Barsac, while Saint-Émilion has its own periodically revised classification. Under the en primeur system, wines are tasted from barrel and sold as futures before they are bottled.
