## Overview
Marche is a central Italian region on the Adriatic coast, running from the sea to the Apennines. It is best known for Verdicchio, one of Italy's most distinctive white grapes, particularly from the hills around Jesi.

## Typical grapes
Verdicchio dominates the whites. Montepulciano and Sangiovese lead the reds. Pecorino and Passerina, two revived local whites, are the specialities of the south around Offida, where Offida Pecorino and Offida Passerina became DOCG wines in 2011.

## Wine styles
Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC is made as a fresh, citrusy white, with a Classico zone, a Superiore level and Riserva, which is a DOCG. Typical notes include lemon, almond and salinity. Rosso Conero must be at least 85% Montepulciano, and its Riserva is the separate Conero DOCG, while Rosso Piceno blends Montepulciano (35 to 85%) with Sangiovese. Sparkling and passito versions of Verdicchio are also made.

## What makes it special
Verdicchio di Matelica, a separate DOC (with a Riserva DOCG) grown further inland in a higher, enclosed valley, tends to be tauter and more intense than the rounder wines of Jesi, which show the Adriatic influence. Verdicchio ages unusually well for an Italian white. Note that Montepulciano here is the grape, not the Tuscan town.
