## Overview
Alentejo is a large, sparsely populated region of southern Portugal, east of Lisbon. Rolling plains, cork oak forests and olive groves surround the vineyards. Wines come under the DOC Alentejo or the broader Vinho Regional Alentejano, and the region is known for some of Portugal's most approachable, popular wines.

## Typical grapes
Reds are led by Aragonez (Tempranillo), Trincadeira and Alicante Bouschet, often blended with Touriga Nacional and Syrah. Whites include Antão Vaz, Arinto and Roupeiro. Antão Vaz is valued for body in the heat, and Arinto for freshness.

## Wine styles
Reds are generally ripe, soft and fruit-forward, with Reserva and Garrafeira bottlings seeing oak. Whites are rounded and fuller than those of the cooler north. The DOC has eight sub-regions that can appear on labels, such as Borba, Évora and Vidigueira.

## What makes it special
Hot, dry summers and varied soils, including schist, granite and clay, shape the wines. Portalegre in the north is higher and cooler, so wines there are fresher. Cork is a major local industry. A tradition dating back to Roman times is vinho de talha, where wine is fermented in large clay pots (talhas); it is still made in places such as Vidigueira and has had its own DOC designation, Alentejo Vinho de Talha, since 2010.
