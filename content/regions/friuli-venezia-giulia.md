## Overview
Friuli-Venezia Giulia is in Italy's far northeast, bordering Austria and Slovenia, with the Adriatic to the south. Despite its modest size, it is regarded as one of Italy's top areas for white wine. Collio and Friuli Colli Orientali (formerly Colli Orientali del Friuli) are the best-known DOCs, while the flat Friuli Grave produces the largest volumes.

## Typical grapes
Friulano (formerly called Tocai Friulano), Ribolla Gialla and Malvasia (Malvasia Istriana) are local whites, alongside Pinot Gris (Pinot Grigio), Sauvignon Blanc and others. Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso is the main native red, while Merlot and Cabernet Franc are widely grown. Schioppettino and Pignolo are local rarities.

## Wine styles
Whites are clean, precise and aromatic, with notable texture in the best examples. Pinot Grigio ranges from simple to complex. Sparkling Ribolla Gialla is increasingly made. Reds are fresh, herbal and savoury, while Ramandolo (from Verduzzo) and Colli Orientali del Friuli Picolit, both DOCGs, are sweet wines, often from late-harvested or dried grapes.

## What makes it special
The Collio and Colli Orientali hills have ponca, layered marl and sandstone, and the Julian Alps shelter vines from the north while the sea moderates the climate. Large temperature swings keep acidity and aromas. The region is also a centre of skin-contact "orange" wine, led by producers such as Gravner and Radikon around Oslavia, near the Slovenian border, who use long macerations and, in Gravner's case, Georgian clay amphorae.
