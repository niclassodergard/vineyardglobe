## Overview
Valais is Switzerland's largest wine canton, a long Alpine valley along the upper Rhône running from around Visp down to Martigny, before the river turns north toward Lake Geneva. It is one of the driest parts of Switzerland, and vines climb steep terraces to high altitudes. Wines are labelled AOC Valais, introduced in 1993, and a dozen communes such as Salgesch, Sion and Vétroz have stricter Grand Cru rules.

## Typical grapes
Chasselas, whose Valais wine is called Fendant, is the most widespread white. Valais is also home to native or traditional varieties such as Petite Arvine, Amigne, Humagne Blanche and Heida (Savagnin), and Cornalin and Humagne Rouge among reds. Pinot Noir and Gamay are the major reds, with Syrah doing well in warm spots.

## Wine styles
Fendant is light, dry and fresh. Dôle is a traditional red blend led by Pinot Noir with Gamay, while Dôle Blanche is a very pale rosé made from the same grapes. Petite Arvine makes dry and sweet wines with citrus and saline notes. Amigne, grown mainly around Vétroz, is famously labelled with one to three bees to show its sweetness. Sweet late-harvest wines and Syrah are also regional specialities.

## What makes it special
Mountains shield the valley from rain, and the foehn wind adds sunshine, producing very warm, dry summers. Vineyards sit on dramatic terraces, including some of the highest in Europe at Visperterminen. Soils vary from schist and granite to limestone and glacial moraine. Ancient irrigation channels (bisses) historically brought water to the vines.
