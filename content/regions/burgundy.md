## Overview
Burgundy (Bourgogne) runs from Chablis in the north to the Mâconnais in the south, with its heart on the Côte d'Or; Beaujolais, further south, is often included in greater Burgundy but has its own identity. It is the benchmark region for Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir produces the great reds of the Côte de Nuits and Côte de Beaune. Chardonnay makes white Burgundy, from steely Chablis to rich Meursault and Puligny-Montrachet. Gamay is the grape of Beaujolais. Aligoté is a minor white.

## Wine styles
Red Burgundy is perfumed, with red-cherry fruit and earthy complexity. White Burgundy ranges from lean and mineral to rich and nutty. Beaujolais is juicy and light, and Crémant de Bourgogne is the sparkling option.

## What makes it special
Burgundy's hierarchy is based on place: regional, village, Premier Cru and Grand Cru vineyards, with Grand Cru wines carrying the vineyard name, such as Romanée-Conti or Montrachet. The Côte d'Or's limestone and marl soils, small plots and inheritance-driven fragmentation mean many growers own tiny parcels. Producers include both domaines and négociants. The concept of climats, recognised by UNESCO, captures the focus on terroir.
