## Overview
Goriška Brda is a hilly region in western Slovenia, on the Italian border. It is the Slovenian continuation of the hills known on the Italian side as Collio, which were divided after the Second World War.

## Typical grapes
Rebula (Ribolla Gialla in Italy) is the flagship white and the most planted grape, covering roughly a fifth to a quarter of the vineyards. Merlot, the leading red, is second, followed by Chardonnay. Other whites include Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Gris (Sivi Pinot) and Friulano, known in Slovenia as Sauvignonasse. Cabernet Franc and Cabernet Sauvignon also appear in red blends.

## Wine styles
Rebula is made in many styles, from crisp dry still and sparkling wines to sweet wines. Skin-contact orange wines, championed by a handful of local producers, are a speciality, and white blends are common. Whites make up the large majority of production.

## What makes it special
Soils are mostly flysch, a layered mix of marl and sandstone known locally as opoka (ponca in Italian), often credited with the wines' mineral character. The Alps shelter the vines while the Adriatic brings warm air, giving a sub-Mediterranean climate. Terraced hillsides, small family estates and a large cooperative cellar, Klet Brda, founded in 1957, shape the region.
