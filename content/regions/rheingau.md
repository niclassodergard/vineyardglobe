## Overview
The Rheingau is a compact region on the north bank of the Rhine, where the river runs east to west near Wiesbaden. It has a long history of quality Riesling and monastic winemaking.

## Typical grapes
Riesling covers most plantings. Spätburgunder (Pinot Noir) is the main red, notably around Assmannshausen.

## Wine styles
Rheingau Rieslings tend to be fuller-bodied and more structured than those of the Mosel. Many are dry (trocken). The Rheingau long had its own Erstes Gewächs term for top dry wines, but since the 2012 vintage VDP members use Grosses Gewächs, like the rest of Germany. Sweet Prädikat wines, including Auslese, Beerenauslese and Eiswein, are also made.

## What makes it special
South-facing slopes beside the river benefit from sunlight and the moderating effect of the water, while the Taunus hills shelter vineyards from cold winds. Soils include loess, loam, quartzite and slate. Historic estates such as Schloss Johannisberg and Kloster Eberbach are tied to the early development of Riesling and of late-harvest wines.
