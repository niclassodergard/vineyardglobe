## Overview
Veneto in northeast Italy is one of the country's largest wine-producing regions by volume. It stretches from Lake Garda and Verona in the west to the plains around Venice, and north into the foothills of the Dolomites.

## Typical grapes
Corvina is the main grape of Valpolicella and Bardolino, blended with Corvinone and Rondinella. Glera is the grape of Prosecco, and Garganega is the principal grape of Soave.

## Wine styles
Valpolicella ranges from light, cherry-scented reds to Ripasso, made by refermenting wine on Amarone pomace. Amarone della Valpolicella is made from grapes dried on racks (appassimento), giving rich, dry, high-alcohol wines. Recioto is the sweet version. Prosecco is typically a fresh, fruity sparkling wine made by the Charmat tank method. Soave is crisp, with almond and citrus notes.

## What makes it special
Prosecco DOC covers a large area extending into neighbouring Friuli, while Conegliano Valdobbiadene is a DOCG from steep hills. Soave Classico comes from the original volcanic and limestone hillside zone. Appassimento gives Amarone its distinctive concentration.
