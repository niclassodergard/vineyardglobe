## Overview
Colchagua Valley is part of the Rapel Valley in Chile's Central Valley, south of Santiago, and is regarded as one of the country's leading areas for premium red wine. It runs from the Andes in the east towards the Pacific in the west.

## Typical grapes
Carmenère, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah are important, with Malbec and Merlot also grown. White grapes, such as Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay, are planted in cooler western and coastal parts.

## Wine styles
Reds are rich, ripe and full-bodied. Carmenère shows dark fruit, spice and herbal notes, and Cabernet and Syrah are powerful. Apalta, a horseshoe-shaped amphitheatre of steep hillside vineyards, is known for deep, concentrated blends.

## What makes it special
Warm, dry summers and low rainfall give consistent ripeness, while the Pacific influence is stronger in the west, around Paredones and Lolol, where cooler conditions suit fresher styles. Granitic hillsides in Apalta and a number of ambitious, investment-driven wineries have raised the valley's profile. Wine tourism, centred on the town of Santa Cruz, is well developed.
