## Overview
The Finger Lakes in upstate New York is a cool-climate region centred on a series of long, deep glacial lakes. It is the state's most prestigious area for vinifera wine, and Riesling is its signature.

## Typical grapes
Riesling leads, from bone-dry to sweet. Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Cabernet Franc are also important, alongside other cool-climate whites such as Gewürztraminer. Blaufränkisch (Lemberger) and Saperavi appear in small amounts, and hybrid and native American varieties are also still grown.

## Wine styles
Riesling is crisp and high in acidity, with citrus and stone-fruit character. There are also late-harvest and ice wines, traditional-method sparkling wines and light, peppery Cabernet Franc.

## What makes it special
The deep lakes retain heat and moderate winter cold and spring frosts, making viticulture viable in a harsh continental climate. Steep slopes beside the lakes and shale-based soils add to the character; Seneca Lake and Cayuga Lake are recognised sub-AVAs. Dr. Konstantin Frank's work in the 1950s showed that European vinifera could survive here, launching the modern industry.
