## Overview
Dão is a Denominação de Origem Controlada in north-central Portugal, around the city of Viseu, ringed by mountains that shelter it from Atlantic rain and continental extremes. It was formally demarcated by a law of 1908, making it one of Portugal's oldest demarcated regions and the first for unfortified wine to be both demarcated and regulated. It is known for elegant, structured reds.

## Typical grapes
Touriga Nacional is the flagship red, with floral and violet notes. Tinta Roriz (Tempranillo), Alfrocheiro and Jaen (the grape Spain calls Mencía) complement it. Encruzado is the most prized white, capable of oak fermentation and ageing, with Malvasia Fina and Bical also important.

## Wine styles
Reds are medium to full bodied with firm tannins, fresh acidity and notable finesse, and the best age well. Whites are dry, textured and mineral. Sparkling wines from Bical and other varieties are also made, with rosé less common. Wines are typically blends of several varieties.

## What makes it special
Many vineyards sit at 400 metres or higher, on granite-derived sandy soils, scattered among pine forests. Mountain ranges such as Caramulo, Buçaco and Serra da Estrela surround the region. For decades cooperatives dominated, but a new generation of independent producers has since driven a quality revival. Seven sub-regions are recognised within the DOC.
