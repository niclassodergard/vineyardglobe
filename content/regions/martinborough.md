## Overview
Martinborough is a small wine town, and a registered Geographical Indication, in the southern Wairarapa near the foot of the North Island, about an hour's drive from Wellington. It is recognised mainly for Pinot Noir and is a boutique region by New Zealand standards; Gladstone, to the north, is a separate Wairarapa sub-region.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir is the flagship variety. Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay, Pinot Gris and Riesling are also grown, with Sauvignon Blanc usually less tropical and more restrained than in Marlborough.

## Wine styles
Pinot Noir here is typically structured and savoury, with dark cherry, plum and herbal notes, and is often more age-worthy than lighter New Zealand examples. Whites are crisp and aromatic, with Pinot Gris and Riesling offering textural, sometimes off-dry styles.

## What makes it special
The core vineyards sit on the Martinborough Terrace, free-draining, stony alluvial gravels laid down by ancient rivers and topped with silty loess, which naturally limit vine vigour. The Remutaka (formerly Rimutaka) and Tararua ranges to the west create a rain shadow, giving a dry climate, while cool nights and strong spring winds lower yields and lengthen the season. Modern viticulture began in the late 1970s and early 1980s with pioneers such as Dry River, Ata Rangi and Martinborough Vineyard, and many producers remain small, family-run and hands-on.
