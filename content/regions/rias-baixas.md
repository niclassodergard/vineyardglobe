## Overview
Rías Baixas is a Denominación de Origen in Galicia, in the green, rainy northwest of Spain. Its vineyards sit near the Atlantic inlets (rías) that give the region its name. It is Spain's benchmark for fresh, aromatic white wine.

## Typical grapes
Albariño (Alvarinho across the border in Portugal) dominates plantings and is the grape behind most wines. Treixadura, Loureiro and Caíño Blanco appear in blends, and the region's few reds, from grapes such as Caíño Tinto, Espadeiro and Sousón, are minor in comparison. A label reading Rías Baixas Albariño must be made entirely from Albariño.

## Wine styles
Albariño is typically dry, high in acidity and aromatic, with citrus, stone fruit and a saline edge. Most is made in stainless steel for early drinking, though some producers age on lees or in oak for more texture. Sparkling and blended styles also exist.

## What makes it special
Cool, humid Atlantic conditions bring disease pressure, so vines are traditionally trained high on pergolas (parras) of granite posts to promote airflow. Plots are extremely fragmented, with many small growers. Granite-based sandy soils are typical. Five sub-zones (Val do Salnés, Condado do Tea, O Rosal, Soutomaior and Ribeira do Ulla) differ in proximity to the sea and in style, with Salnés the coolest and most maritime and Condado do Tea, inland along the Miño, the warmest. Seafood pairs naturally with the wines.
