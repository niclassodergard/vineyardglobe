## Overview
The Mornington Peninsula is a Geographical Indication in Victoria, south-east of Melbourne, on a peninsula between Port Phillip Bay and Western Port. Its vineyards are mostly small and boutique, and the region is known for cool-climate Pinot Noir and Chardonnay.

## Typical grapes
Pinot Noir is the most planted variety, followed by Chardonnay and Pinot Gris. Small amounts of Syrah (Shiraz in Australia) are made in warmer sites, along with some Sauvignon Blanc and other varieties.

## Wine styles
Pinot Noir ranges from bright red cherry and spice to more structured, savoury wines in warmer sites. Chardonnay is typically restrained, with citrus and stone fruit, fine acidity and subtle oak, often with some lees and barrel work. Pinot Gris can be fresh and textured. Syrah is peppery and medium-bodied.

## What makes it special
Water on three sides moderates temperatures, and sea breezes and a long, cool ripening season preserve acidity. There are no official sub-regions, but locals distinguish the higher, cooler and wetter "up the hill" sites around Red Hill, Main Ridge and Merricks, on red volcanic soils, from the lower, warmer and sandier areas around Moorooduc and Tuerong in the north. Most producers are small and family-run, and the region's proximity to Melbourne makes cellar doors and food tourism a major part of its identity.
