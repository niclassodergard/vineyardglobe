## Overview
Nebbiolo is the grape of Barolo and Barbaresco, two of Italy's most celebrated and long-lived red wines. It is a demanding variety that does best on a few selected sites.

## Origin and history
Nebbiolo is native to northwest Italy, with documented references from the Middle Ages. Its name is often linked to nebbia, the autumn fog of the Piedmont hills, or to nobile, though neither is certain. It ripens late, usually well into October, and is hard to grow successfully elsewhere. Outside Italy only small plantings exist, such as in California, Australia and Argentina.

## Flavour and structure
Nebbiolo makes wines that are pale in colour but powerful, with high acidity and high, grippy tannin. Classic aromas are rose, tar, red cherry, dried herbs and liquorice, with truffle and leather developing with age. Soil and site have a strong effect on the wine's style and weight.

## Styles and ageing
Barolo and Barbaresco are made under strict rules with required ageing in oak and bottle. Lighter wines include Nebbiolo d'Alba and Langhe Nebbiolo, as well as Gattinara, Ghemme and Valtellina (where it is called Chiavennasca). Traditional Barolo can need a decade or more to soften, while modern approaches make wines accessible earlier. Great vintages can age for several decades.
