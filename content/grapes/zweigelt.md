## Overview
Zweigelt is Austria's most widely planted red grape. It makes fruity, approachable wines, and it is grown mainly in Niederösterreich and Burgenland.

## Origin and history
Zweigelt is a modern crossing of Blaufränkisch and St. Laurent, made in 1922 by Fritz Zweigelt at the viticultural institute in Klosterneuburg, near Vienna. It was first called Rotburger, which remains a synonym. Its spread after the Second World War owed much to the influential grower Lenz Moser, who planted it widely and promoted the name Zweigelt, officially adopted in the 1970s. Because Fritz Zweigelt was an early Nazi party member, campaigners have called since 2018 for the grape to be renamed.

## Flavour and structure
Zweigelt shows sour cherry, raspberry and plum, with a peppery, spicy edge. Tannins are soft to medium and acidity medium, and the wines are medium-bodied. Yields are high, so careful vineyard work is needed: restrained yields give darker, more concentrated wines.

## Styles and ageing
Most Zweigelt is made for youthful drinking, fresh, juicy and unoaked or only lightly oaked. More serious versions, aged in barrel, are fuller and spicier and can improve for five to ten years. It is often blended with Blaufränkisch, St. Laurent or Cabernet Sauvignon, and it also makes rosé and, less commonly, sweet wines.
