## Overview
Blaufränkisch is a dark-skinned red grape of Central Europe, making peppery, savoury wines with firm acidity. It is called Lemberger in Germany, Kékfrankos in Hungary, Frankovka in the Czech Republic and Slovakia, and Modra Frankinja in Slovenia.

## Origin and history
DNA work shows Blaufränkisch is a natural cross of Gouais Blanc (Heunisch Weiss) and Blaue Zimmettraube. Its likely birthplace is Lower Styria, now in Slovenia, where Blaue Zimmettraube was grown, and it was first documented under its current name in Vienna in 1862, though it is probably much older. Despite old Gamay-like names in some countries, it is not related to Gamay. Blaufränkisch is a parent of Zweigelt and several other Austrian crossings, and it is most important in Burgenland, Hungary's Sopron and Szekszárd, and Württemberg.

## Flavour and structure
Typical flavours are blackberry, sour cherry, dark plum, black pepper and spice, sometimes with a mineral or earthy edge. Acidity is high, tannin medium to high and body medium. Cooler sites give a tense, peppery profile, while warmer ones give riper, plusher fruit.

## Styles and ageing
It is made as juicy, young reds, but the best, often oaked, Burgenland examples from Mittelburgenland and Leithaberg are age-worthy for ten years or more. It is often blended with Zweigelt, Cabernet Sauvignon or Merlot, and it also makes rosé.
