## Overview
Tannat is a late-ripening, thick-skinned red grape renowned for very deep colour and powerful tannins. It is the backbone of Madiran in south-west France and is regarded as the national grape of Uruguay.

## Origin and history
Tannat comes from the foothills of the Pyrenees in south-west France, and its name is often linked to its tannins, though this is not certain. Its full parentage is unknown, but DNA studies have found a close, probably parent-offspring, relationship with the rare local variety Manseng Noir. It reached Uruguay in the 1870s, traditionally credited to the Basque settler Pascual Harriague, and the grape is still sometimes called Harriague there.

## Flavour and structure
Tannat gives inky wines with blackberry, plum, violet, liquorice and tobacco notes. Tannins are very high and acidity is medium-high, so young wines can be austere and rustic. Warmer sites give riper fruit and softer texture, while cool, wet vintages emphasise green, grippy tannin.

## Styles and ageing
Madiran must now contain 60 to 80 percent Tannat, blended with Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon or Fer Servadou to temper its structure, and winemakers often use gentle extraction or micro-oxygenation, a technique developed in Madiran, to soften the tannins. In Uruguay it appears both as a varietal red and in blends, with a range from fruity everyday wines to oaked, concentrated bottlings. Good examples can age for a decade or more.
