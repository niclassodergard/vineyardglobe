## Overview
Garganega is the principal white grape of Soave and Gambellara in the Veneto. It makes elegant, almond-scented wines that range from simple and crisp to complex and long-lived.

## Origin and history
Garganega has been documented in the Veneto for several centuries, with early references dating back to the Middle Ages. DNA studies published in 2003 and 2008 showed it has the same profile as Grecanico Dorato, grown in Sicily, so they are one variety under two names. Its own parents are unknown, but the 2008 study found first-degree relationships with many Italian varieties, such as Trebbiano Toscano, making it a key figure in Italy's grape family tree. The name Garganega is not used for the Sicilian wines, which are labelled Grecanico.

## Flavour and structure
Garganega shows white peach, pear, lemon and white flowers, with a characteristic almond note. Acidity is medium-high and body medium. It is a vigorous, late-ripening variety: high yields give neutral wines, while low yields on volcanic or limestone hillsides give concentrated, mineral and honeyed wines.

## Styles and ageing
Soave is made with at least 70 percent Garganega, often blended with Trebbiano di Soave. Soave Classico, from the original hillside zone, is usually richer than wines from the plain. Garganega is also used to make the sweet Recioto di Soave and Recioto di Gambellara from dried grapes. Good dry wines can age for 5 to 10 years, gaining nutty, waxy and honeyed notes.
