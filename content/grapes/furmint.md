## Overview
Furmint is the principal white grape of Tokaj in Hungary, where it forms the backbone of the sweet Tokaji Aszú wines. It is also increasingly made as a dry white, prized for its high acidity and ability to reflect its soil.

## Origin and history
Furmint is a Hungarian variety closely tied to Tokaj, and DNA analysis has shown a parent-offspring link with Gouais Blanc, an old and widely spread variety. Furmint is most likely the offspring. It is also closely related to Hárslevelű, its usual partner in Tokaj. Tokaj's sweet wines were famous by the 18th century, a royal decree of 1737 fixed the region's boundaries, and its vineyards were ranked into classes during the same century. The grape is also grown in Austria (as Mosler) and in Slovenia and Croatia, where Šipon and Moslavac are names for it.

## Flavour and structure
Furmint is high in acidity with medium body. Typical flavours include green apple, pear, quince and lemon, with smoke and mineral notes from volcanic soils. It buds early, ripens late and is prone to botrytis, which is vital for sweet wine.

## Styles and ageing
Furmint makes dry, sweet and sparkling wines. Tokaji Aszú is made from botrytised berries and is extremely long-lived. Dry examples vary from fresh and unoaked to richer, barrel-fermented wines that can age for a decade or more.
