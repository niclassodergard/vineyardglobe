## Overview
Mourvèdre is a late-ripening, dark-skinned red grape that gives structured, tannic and long-lived wines. It is an important part of southern French and Spanish red blends.

## Origin and history
Mourvèdre is thought to come from Spain's Mediterranean coast, where it is known as Monastrell. Its French name probably derives from Murviedro (Sagunto) near Valencia, and Mataro from the town of Mataró near Barcelona. It is grown extensively in Valencia, Jumilla and Yecla. In France it is best known in Bandol, in Provence, and in the southern Rhône and Languedoc. In Australia and California it has traditionally been called Mataro. It needs warmth and a long season to ripen, so plantings are limited to hot, sunny sites.

## Flavour and structure
Mourvèdre has dark fruit such as blackberry and plum, with black pepper, dried herbs and savoury notes of game, leather and meat. It has firm, grippy tannin, medium acidity and often high alcohol. It can show reductive, earthy aromas in youth.

## Styles and ageing
Bandol reds must contain at least half Mourvèdre and spend at least 18 months in oak, usually large casks; they can age for 15 years or more. In Châteauneuf-du-Pape and across Australia it is blended with Grenache and Syrah (the "GSM" blend), adding structure and depth. It also makes dark, powerful Monastrell wines in Spain, and is used for rosé, particularly in Bandol.
