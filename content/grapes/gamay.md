## Overview
Gamay is a red grape best known as the grape of Beaujolais, in the south of Burgundy. It makes light, fruity, low-tannin reds that are usually enjoyed young.

## Origin and history
Gamay is generally thought to take its name from the hamlet of Gamay, near Saint-Aubin in the Côte de Beaune. In July 1395 Duke Philip the Bold of Burgundy condemned the "disloyal" Gamay and ordered it uprooted in favour of Pinot Noir. It went on to thrive further south in Beaujolais, where granite soils suit it. DNA analysis shows Gamay is a natural cross of Pinot and Gouais Blanc, making it a sibling of Chardonnay. It is also grown in the Loire Valley, Switzerland and Oregon.

## Flavour and structure
Gamay shows red cherry, raspberry and strawberry, with violet and peony. Wines made by carbonic maceration add banana and bubblegum notes. Acidity is medium to high, tannin is low and body is light to medium. Wines from the granite slopes of the Beaujolais crus tend to have more structure.

## Styles and ageing
Beaujolais Nouveau is released in the November after harvest. Beaujolais-Villages is fruity and simple. The ten Beaujolais crus, such as Morgon, Moulin-à-Vent and Fleurie, are more structured and can age for 5 to 10 years or longer, taking on Pinot Noir-like earthy character.
