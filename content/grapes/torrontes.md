## Overview
Torrontés is Argentina's signature white grape, producing strongly aromatic, floral wines. The name covers three related Argentine varieties, of which Torrontés Riojano is the most planted and gives the finest wines.

## Origin and history
The three Argentine Torrontés are Riojano, Sanjuanino and Mendocino, named after the provinces of La Rioja, San Juan and Mendoza. DNA studies published in 2003 showed that Torrontés Riojano is a natural cross of Muscat of Alexandria and Criolla Chica, the grape also known as Listán Prieto, Mission and País, which the Spanish brought to the Americas. Torrontés Sanjuanino has the same parents, while Torrontés Mendocino shares Muscat of Alexandria as one parent, with the other unidentified. The grapes therefore arose in the Americas, most likely in Argentina, and are not related to the Spanish Torrontés of Galicia.

## Flavour and structure
Torrontés smells like Muscat: peach, apricot, rose petal, orange blossom and lychee, with citrus peel. Despite its perfume, the palate is usually dry. Acidity is medium and body medium, and cool, high-altitude sites in Salta preserve freshness, while warm sites can give flabby, bitter finishes.

## Styles and ageing
Most Torrontés is dry, unoaked and made in stainless steel for early drinking, ideally within two or three years. The best-known source is the high-altitude Cafayate area of Salta. Late-harvest and sparkling versions also exist.
