## Overview
Merlot is a red grape known for soft tannins, plummy fruit and a supple, approachable texture. It is among the most planted grapes in the world and is the leading variety on the Right Bank of Bordeaux.

## Origin and history
Merlot comes from south-west France, and DNA profiling has shown that Cabernet Franc is one of its parents; the other is a rare variety called Magdeleine Noire des Charentes. It was first recorded in 1784, as Merlau, in the Libournais. Its name is thought to derive from the French word for blackbird, merle. It is the main grape of Saint-Émilion and Pomerol, and later became popular in regions such as California, Chile, northern Italy and Washington State.

## Flavour and structure
Merlot ripens earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon and has thinner skins, giving lower tannin and a rounder mouthfeel. Typical flavours are plum, black and red cherry and chocolate. Cooler sites show more herbal and red-fruit notes, while warmer sites give riper, jammier fruit and higher alcohol. It does well on cool, water-retentive clay soils, as in Pomerol, and on the limestone of Saint-Émilion.

## Styles and ageing
Merlot is made as an easy-drinking varietal wine and is also an important blending partner, often with Cabernet Sauvignon and Cabernet Franc. Top examples from Pomerol and Saint-Émilion are rich, oak-aged and can age for decades, developing truffle, leather and dried fruit notes.
