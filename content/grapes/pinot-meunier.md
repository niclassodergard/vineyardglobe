## Overview
Pinot Meunier is a dark-skinned grape best known as the third pillar of Champagne, alongside Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. It is valued for fruity, supple wines that give blends early charm. In Germany it is called Schwarzriesling or Müllerrebe and is also made as a still red.

## Origin and history
Pinot Meunier is a mutation of Pinot Noir, and it is a chimera: its outer cell layer (L1) carries the mutation, while the inner layers resemble Pinot Noir. The name comes from the French word for miller, referring to the flour-like white down on the young leaves; the German Müllerrebe has the same meaning. It is usually said to have been recorded from the sixteenth century. Because it buds later and ripens earlier than Pinot Noir, it is less exposed to spring frost, which made it popular in the cold, frost-prone Marne Valley.

## Flavour and structure
Meunier gives red apple, cherry, red berry and plum fruit, with soft spice and earthy notes. Compared with Pinot Noir it is usually fruitier and less structured, with medium-high acidity, light tannin and a medium body. Its colour is often paler and its wines mature earlier.

## Styles and ageing
In Champagne it adds roundness and fresh fruit to blends and a growing number of growers bottle it on its own, as a Blanc de Noirs made entirely from Meunier. Still versions from Germany, especially Württemberg, and from Australia and California are light to medium-bodied reds. Most Meunier is best drunk young, though top Champagne blends can age for many years.
