## Overview
Macabeo is one of Spain's most widely planted white grapes. It is known as Viura in Rioja and Macabeu in Catalan, and is a classic component of Cava.

## Origin and history
Macabeo is generally considered Spanish, most probably from Catalonia, where the agronomist Miquel Agustí recorded it in 1617 in his book on agriculture, although some writers suggest the wider Ebro Valley. It spread widely across Spain and into southern France, where it is a permitted grape in Roussillon and the Languedoc. In Rioja its importance grew after phylloxera and it became the region's main white grape.

## Flavour and structure
Macabeo is fairly neutral, with green apple, lemon, white flowers, melon and a faint almond or fennel note. Acidity is medium, body medium and alcohol moderate. The vine is productive, so yields must be controlled to avoid dilute wines.

## Styles and ageing
In Cava it is blended with Xarel·lo and Parellada, contributing freshness, delicate fruit and floral notes. In Rioja, fresh unoaked Viura is crisp and light, while barrel-fermented and long-aged white Rioja develops nutty, honeyed, waxy complexity and can age for decades. It also appears in still blends and in some sweet wines elsewhere in Spain and France.
