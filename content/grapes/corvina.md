## Overview
Corvina, formally Corvina Veronese, is the leading red grape of the Verona area in Veneto. It is the main component of Valpolicella, Amarone della Valpolicella, Recioto and Bardolino, where it gives bright cherry fruit and fresh acidity.

## Origin and history
Corvina is native to the Verona hills and Lake Garda area, and the earliest reliable written reference is usually dated to 1627. Its own parents are not established, but DNA profiling indicates that it is a parent of Rondinella, its usual blending partner. Corvinone, long thought to be a clone of Corvina, was shown by DNA profiling to be a separate variety, and the two are best not confused. Corvina has a thick skin and is traditionally trained on the pergola veronese.

## Flavour and structure
Corvina shows sour and red cherry, almond and light spice, with high acidity, modest tannin and medium body. Alone it can lack depth and colour, so it is blended, most often with Rondinella. Valpolicella rules require Corvina and Corvinone together to make up 45 to 95 percent of the blend, with 5 to 30 percent Rondinella.

## Styles and ageing
Simple Valpolicella is light, fresh and drunk young. Ripasso is refermented on Amarone skins for more body. In Amarone and Recioto, grapes are dried for months before fermentation, giving concentrated, powerful wines with dried fruit, spice and bitter-cherry notes. Amarone can age for well over a decade, while Recioto is sweet.
