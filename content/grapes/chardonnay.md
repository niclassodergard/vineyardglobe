## Overview
Chardonnay is one of the world's most widely planted white grapes and a favourite of winemakers because it adapts to almost any climate. It makes everything from lean, steely Chablis to rich, oaked wines, and it is one of the main grapes of Champagne.

## Origin and history
Chardonnay originated in north-eastern France, and DNA profiling published in 1999 showed it is a natural cross of Pinot and Gouais Blanc, making it a sibling of Aligoté, Gamay and Melon. It is closely tied to Burgundy, and spread worldwide through the twentieth century, notably to California, Australia and Chile.

## Flavour and structure
The grape itself is fairly neutral, so style depends on site and winemaking. Cool climates give green apple, citrus and a mineral edge with high acidity. Warmer sites give peach, melon and tropical fruit with fuller body. Malolactic conversion adds buttery notes, and oak adds vanilla, toast and hazelnut.

## Styles and ageing
Styles range from unoaked, crisp wines to barrel-fermented and lees-aged examples, plus sparkling wine, including Blanc de Blancs. Fine white Burgundy can age for a decade or more, gaining nutty, honeyed complexity, while simple unoaked wines are best drunk young.
