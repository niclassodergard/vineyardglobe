## Overview
Pinot Noir is a thin-skinned red grape that produces some of the world's most prized and most expensive wines. It is the great red grape of Burgundy and a major component of Champagne.

## Origin and history
Pinot Noir is one of the oldest cultivated grape varieties, with a long record in Burgundy, where Cistercian monks helped define its best sites. DNA work shows that Pinot is a parent of many varieties, including Chardonnay, Aligoté and Gamay through crossings with Gouais Blanc. It is notoriously prone to mutation, giving colour variants such as Pinot Gris and Pinot Blanc, which are genetically the same variety. Today it is grown in Germany, Switzerland, Italy, Oregon, California, New Zealand, Australia and Chile.

## Flavour and structure
Typical aromas are red cherry, raspberry and strawberry, with violet, forest floor and mushroom emerging with age. Acidity is high, tannin is low to medium and body is light to medium. Cool climates give tart red fruit and savoury notes; warmer sites give riper, darker fruit and fuller body. Thin skins and tight bunches make it prone to rot, early budding exposes it to spring frost, and quality suffers at high yields, so site choice matters greatly.

## Styles and ageing
Pinot Noir is made as still red wine, rosé and, with Chardonnay, as a base for Champagne and other sparkling wines. The best reds can age for 10 to 20 years or more, developing savoury, earthy complexity. Oak is used in moderation to avoid overwhelming its delicate fruit.
