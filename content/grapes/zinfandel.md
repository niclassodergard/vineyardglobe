## Overview
Zinfandel is best known as California's historic red grape, producing ripe, fruity and often high-alcohol wines. In southern Italy the same variety is called Primitivo.

## Origin and history
DNA profiling by Carole Meredith's team showed in the early 1990s that Zinfandel and Primitivo are the same variety, and in 2001 matched them to the Croatian grape Crljenak Kaštelanski. Its older Croatian name, Tribidrag, is recorded as early as the 15th century. It is therefore native to Croatia's Dalmatian coast. It reached the eastern United States around 1829 and was planted widely in California from the 1850s, where old head-trained vines remain. It also reached Puglia in southern Italy, where it was named Primitivo for its early ripening.

## Flavour and structure
Zinfandel ripens unevenly, so bunches can contain both raisined and underripe berries. Wines show blackberry, raspberry jam, black pepper and spice, with medium tannin, medium acidity and a full body. Alcohol is often high, commonly 14 percent or more.

## Styles and ageing
Styles include dry red Zinfandel, Puglian Primitivo such as Primitivo di Manduria, and the pink, off-dry White Zinfandel popular in the United States. Late-harvest and port-style wines are also made. Most wines are best enjoyed within several years, though old-vine examples from good producers can age longer.
