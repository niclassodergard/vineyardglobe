## Overview
Touriga Nacional is widely considered Portugal's finest red grape. It makes concentrated, floral, structured wines and is a key component of both vintage Port and modern Douro and Dão table wines.

## Origin and history
Its homeland is debated, with the Dão and the Douro both cited, though many authorities favour the Dão, where it was once known as Preto Mortágua. Low yields and small berries made it commercially unattractive for much of the twentieth century, and plantings declined. Research and selection from the later twentieth century revived it, and it is now planted well beyond Portugal. DNA profiling shows it is a parent of Touriga Franca, a natural cross with Marufo.

## Flavour and structure
The grape shows blackberry, blueberry and black plum, with violet, bergamot and rockrose-like floral and herbal notes. Tannins are firm and plentiful, acidity is medium-high and the body is full. Hotter sites deliver darker, riper fruit, while cooler, higher sites keep more perfume and freshness.

## Styles and ageing
In the Douro it is blended with Touriga Franca, Tinta Roriz and others for Port and dry reds, while in the Dão it is paired with Jaen, Alfrocheiro and Tinta Roriz. Single-variety bottlings are increasingly common. Wines are built to age, with top examples developing for 10 to 20 years.
