## Overview
Cabernet Sauvignon is one of the world's most widely planted red grapes. It makes deeply coloured, tannic wines with great ageing potential, and it is the defining grape of the Médoc and many New World regions.

## Origin and history
DNA analysis at the University of California, Davis, in 1997 showed that Cabernet Sauvignon is a natural crossing of Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc, which most likely occurred spontaneously in the Bordeaux area. It is recorded there in the 18th century, first under names such as Petite Vidure, and it rose to prominence in the Médoc and Graves in the 18th and 19th centuries. It then spread across the globe, helped by its adaptability, and today thrives in places such as California, Chile, Australia and South Africa.

## Flavour and structure
Small, thick-skinned berries give high tannin, deep colour and medium-high acidity. Typical aromas are blackcurrant, black cherry, cedar and graphite, with green pepper notes when grapes are underripe. Cooler climates give more herbal, leaner wines; warmer climates give riper, plusher black fruit and higher alcohol. The grape ripens late, so it needs warmth.

## Styles and ageing
It is made as a varietal wine or, classically, blended with Merlot and Cabernet Franc in the Bordeaux style. Oak ageing adds vanilla, spice and cedar. Good examples can age for decades, with tannins softening and flavours of tobacco, leather and earth developing.
