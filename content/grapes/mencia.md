## Overview
Mencía is a red grape of north-western Spain that makes aromatic, fresh, mineral-tinged wines. It is the leading red variety of Bierzo and an important one in Ribeira Sacra, Valdeorras and Monterrei.

## Origin and history
For years Mencía was thought to be a clone of Cabernet Franc, but DNA profiling showed they are not related. Instead it is identical to Jaen, the grape grown in Portugal's Dão region. Whether it began in north-west Spain or northern Portugal is debated. DNA parentage analysis indicates that it is a natural cross of Alfrocheiro and Patorra, two old Iberian varieties. Interest grew from the late 1990s as growers in Bierzo revived old, low-yielding bush vines on slate slopes.

## Flavour and structure
Mencía shows red cherry, raspberry and blackberry with violet, liquorice, herbs and a stony or graphite note. Acidity is medium-high, tannins are medium and the body is medium, becoming fuller with riper fruit and old vines. Cooler, higher sites give a more floral, lifted style.

## Styles and ageing
Young, unoaked wines are juicy and easy-drinking. Wines from old vines and steep slate or granite terraces, often aged in oak, are deeper and more complex. In Dão, Jaen is made both as a varietal wine and as part of blends. Good examples can age for 5 to 10 years or more.
