## Overview
Albariño is an Atlantic-influenced white grape known for bright acidity, aromatic peach and citrus fruit and a saline edge. It is the signature white of Galicia in north-west Spain, where it dominates the Rías Baixas DO.

## Origin and history
The same grape is called Alvarinho in northern Portugal, where it is central to Vinho Verde from Monção e Melgaço. An old story says that monks from Cluny brought it from Germany or Burgundy in the 12th century, but there is no evidence for this, and the grape is now thought to be native to Galicia and northern Portugal. Its parentage is not established. Rías Baixas, created as a DO in 1988, helped to raise its international profile, and plantings have appeared in California, Australia and Uruguay.

## Flavour and structure
Typical wines show peach, apricot, lemon and grapefruit with floral notes and often a salty, mineral finish. Acidity is high, alcohol is moderate, and the thick skins and small berries give some texture. Wet Atlantic conditions encourage training on high pergolas to reduce rot.

## Styles and ageing
Most Albariño is dry, unoaked and made to be drunk young and fresh. Lees ageing adds texture, and some producers use oak or concrete for more complexity. Top examples can develop honeyed, nutty notes over several years.
