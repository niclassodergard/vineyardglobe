## Overview
Sangiovese is Italy's most widely planted red grape and the heart of Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. It is also grown in Corsica, where it is known as Nielluccio.

## Origin and history
Sangiovese has grown in central Italy for centuries, and its name is often traced to the Latin sanguis Jovis, "blood of Jove", though this is unproven. DNA research suggests it is a cross of Ciliegiolo and Calabrese di Montenuovo, a little-known variety from southern Italy. It exists in many clones and local forms, known as Brunello in Montalcino and Prugnolo Gentile in Montepulciano; the old split into "Grosso" and "Piccolo" types is no longer seen as meaningful.

## Flavour and structure
Sangiovese is typically high in acidity, with firm, dusty tannin and a medium body. Sour cherry and red plum are the core flavours, with dried herbs, tomato leaf and violet. Warmer sites and clones give riper fruit and more weight, while cooler sites are leaner and more savoury. Colour is often fairly pale.

## Styles and ageing
Wines range from fresh, youthful Chianti and Morellino di Scansano to age-worthy Chianti Classico Riserva and Brunello, which cannot be released until about five years after the vintage. It is blended with local grapes such as Canaiolo or with international varieties in some Tuscan wines. Top examples develop leather, tea and dried cherry notes over a decade or more.
