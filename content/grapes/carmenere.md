## Overview
Carmenère is a red grape with spicy, dark-fruited flavours and a distinctive savoury, peppery character. Once a Bordeaux variety, it is now most closely associated with Chile.

## Origin and history
DNA analysis shows Carmenère is an offspring of Cabernet Franc and Gros Cabernet, an old variety from south-west France. It was widely planted in Bordeaux before the phylloxera crisis in the 19th century, after which it was largely abandoned because it flowers poorly and ripens late. Cuttings reached Chile in the 19th century, where it was long mistaken for Merlot. The French ampelographer Jean-Michel Boursiquot identified it there in 1994, and Chile officially recognised it in 1998. DNA work suggests that China's Cabernet Gernischt is most likely Carmenère too.

## Flavour and structure
Carmenère needs a long, warm season to ripen fully. Underripe fruit gives green, bell pepper flavours; ripe fruit shows blackberry, dark chocolate, spice and tobacco. Tannins are soft to medium, and acidity is moderate, giving a rounded, smooth style.

## Styles and ageing
It is made as a varietal wine in Chile, notably in the Colchagua and Cachapoal valleys (both part of Rapel) and in Maipo, and is also blended with Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot. Most wines are best drunk within several years, although concentrated, oak-aged examples can age longer.
