## Overview
Pinot Blanc is a white grape that makes fresh, dry, gently aromatic wines with a soft, creamy texture. It is a quiet, food-friendly variety rather than a showy one, and it is important in Alsace, Germany, Austria and northern Italy.

## Origin and history
Pinot Blanc is a colour mutation within the Pinot family, arising from Pinot Noir, probably via Pinot Gris, and most likely in Burgundy. For a long time it was confused with Chardonnay: the ampelographer Victor Pulliat distinguished the two in 1868, but in some countries they were still lumped together well into the 20th century. It is known as Weissburgunder in Germany and Austria, and Pinot Bianco in Italy. In Alsace the old name Klevner is still used, and wine labelled Pinot Blanc there often includes the related Auxerrois. Elsewhere, "Clevner" can refer to other Pinot varieties.

## Flavour and structure
The wines are typically dry and medium-bodied, with apple, pear, citrus, melon and almond notes. Acidity is usually medium, tannin is absent, and the flavours are subtle rather than overtly fruity. Warmer sites bring riper, rounder fruit and higher alcohol, while cooler sites keep it crisp and fresh.

## Styles and ageing
Most Pinot Blanc is unoaked and made to drink young. Some producers use oak or lees contact for more texture, in a style reminiscent of Chardonnay. It is also a major grape for sparkling wines, notably Crémant d'Alsace. Good examples, especially from Alsace and Alto Adige, can age for five to ten years.
