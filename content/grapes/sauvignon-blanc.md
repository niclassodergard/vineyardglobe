## Overview
Sauvignon Blanc is an aromatic, high-acid white grape known for vivid, herbaceous and fruity wines. It is grown across the world and produces both crisp, refreshing wines and richer oaked blends.

## Origin and history
It comes from western France, with the Loire Valley and Bordeaux the traditional homes; early records, including Rabelais in 1534, favour the Loire. Its name is thought to derive from the French word sauvage, meaning wild. DNA studies show it is an offspring of Savagnin (Traminer) and a parent of Cabernet Sauvignon, which resulted from a cross with Cabernet Franc. Its modern worldwide fame owes much to New Zealand's Marlborough region from the 1980s.

## Flavour and structure
Typical aromas include gooseberry, grapefruit, passion fruit, cut grass and elderflower. Pyrazines give green, herbaceous notes, which dominate in cool climates and when grapes are picked early. Warmer climates and riper fruit give tropical flavours. Acidity is high and body light to medium.

## Styles and ageing
Most Sauvignon Blanc is unoaked, stainless-steel fermented and drunk young, as in Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé. In Bordeaux it is often blended with Sémillon, sometimes with oak, and it is the junior partner to Sémillon in sweet wines such as Sauternes. Oaked examples are sometimes labelled Fumé Blanc, a name coined in California by Robert Mondavi in the late 1960s.
