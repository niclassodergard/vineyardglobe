## Overview
Carignan is a dark-skinned red grape that is vigorous, late-ripening and naturally high in acidity and tannin. Once planted widely for volume, it has regained respect as the source of intense wines from old, low-yielding vines.

## Origin and history
Carignan is generally thought to originate in north-eastern Spain, probably in Aragón, and its Spanish name is usually linked to the town of Cariñena, although this rests on the name rather than firm documentary proof. It spread to Catalonia, Sardinia, southern France and North Africa. In the Languedoc it was planted for high yields, and around the late 1980s it was France's most widely planted grape, until European vine-pull schemes cut its area roughly in half and Merlot overtook it. It is called Cariñena or Samsó in Catalonia, Mazuelo in Rioja, and Carignano or Bovale Grande in Sardinia.

## Flavour and structure
Expect dark cherry, blackberry, dried herbs, spice and a savoury, earthy edge. Acidity and tannin are high, and colour is deep. High yields give coarse, rustic wines, while old bush vines give concentrated, balanced fruit.

## Styles and ageing
Carignan is often blended with Grenache, Syrah and Mourvèdre to add colour, acidity and structure, and it is also bottled as a varietal wine, notably from old vines in the Languedoc, Priorat, Sardinia and Chile. Carignan made by carbonic maceration is softer and fruitier. Old-vine examples can age for 10 years or more.
