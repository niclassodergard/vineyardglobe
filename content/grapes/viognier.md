## Overview
Viognier is an aromatic white grape that gives rich, perfumed wines with a full, viscous texture. It is low in acidity and can reach high alcohol levels.

## Origin and history
The grape is associated with the northern Rhône, where it is the sole variety of Condrieu and Château-Grillet. DNA profiling suggests a parent–offspring relationship with Mondeuse Blanche from Savoie, which is also a parent of Syrah, and a close link with Piedmont's Freisa. It was once nearly lost, with only about a dozen hectares left in France by the mid-1960s, before its revival in the Rhône, the Languedoc and then California, Australia and other warm regions.

## Flavour and structure
Peach, apricot and honeysuckle dominate, often with floral notes of orange blossom and violet. Acidity is modest, and the body is full and rounded. It needs ripeness to develop its aromatics but can turn heavy and oily if picked too late. Cooler picks keep more freshness and citrus.

## Styles and ageing
Most Viognier is dry and is made in stainless steel or with restrained oak for extra richness. Some producers also make late-harvest sweet wines. A small amount is co-fermented with Syrah in Côte-Rôtie to add perfume and colour stability. Most are best drunk young while the fruit is vivid, though top Condrieu can age for several years.
