## Overview
Malbec is a deeply coloured red grape producing rich, fruity wines with ripe tannins. It is the flagship grape of Argentina and also the traditional grape of Cahors in France.

## Origin and history
Malbec originated in south-west France, where it is known as Côt or Auxerrois. DNA studies identified its parents as Prunelard and Magdeleine Noire des Charentes, making it a half-sibling of Merlot. It was once widely grown in Bordeaux but declined after the severe frost of 1956 and because of its susceptibility to coulure and rot. In 1853 the French agronomist Michel Pouget brought cuttings to Argentina, where the grape found ideal conditions in Mendoza. Its international rise came from the 1990s, as Argentine producers turned to quality.

## Flavour and structure
Malbec has thick, dark skins that give inky colour and firm tannins. Typical flavours are blackberry, plum and violet, with cocoa and spice. Argentine wines tend to be riper and rounder, while Cahors is more rustic, tannic and earthy. High-altitude vineyards keep acidity fresh and extend ripening.

## Styles and ageing
It is made as a vibrant, fruit-forward wine for early drinking, and as oak-aged, concentrated wines from top sites such as Luján de Cuyo and the Uco Valley. In Cahors it was long known as "black wine" and can age for many years. It is also used in Bordeaux-style blends.
