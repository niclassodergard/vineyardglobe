## Overview
Pinotage is South Africa's signature red grape, a variety created in the country in the twentieth century. It is made into everything from easy fruity reds to serious, oaked wines.

## Origin and history
Pinotage was bred in 1925 by Abraham Perold, a professor at Stellenbosch University, who crossed Pinot Noir with Cinsault, then known locally as Hermitage. The name combines Pinot and Hermitage. The first wine was made in 1941, and the first wine labelled Pinotage, the 1959 vintage from Bellevue, was released under the Lanzerac label in 1961. It later became a symbol of the South African industry. Small plantings exist in New Zealand, California and elsewhere.

## Flavour and structure
Pinotage is deeply coloured, with medium acidity and fairly firm tannin. Typical flavours are blackberry, plum and red cherry with smoky, earthy notes. Some wines show banana-like or acetone (paint) notes, often attributed to warm or stressed fermentations. Better winemaking and lower yields give cleaner, more complex wines.

## Styles and ageing
Styles include fresh, unoaked reds, oaked and barrel-aged wines, Cape blends mixing Pinotage with Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, and rosé and sparkling wines. Top bottlings from old bush vines can age well for ten years or more.
