## Overview
Tempranillo is Spain's flagship red grape and the backbone of Rioja and Ribera del Duero. It is also important in Portugal, where it is called Tinta Roriz or Aragonez.

## Origin and history
Tempranillo is Iberian in origin. DNA analysis indicates it is a cross of Albillo Mayor and Benedicto, two Spanish varieties. Its name comes from the Spanish temprano, meaning early, because it ripens earlier than many neighbouring varieties. It has spread widely under local names and is now planted in Argentina, Australia and the United States as well.

## Flavour and structure
Tempranillo gives medium-acid wines with moderate to firm tannin and a medium to full body. Cooler, higher sites tend to show red cherry, plum and fresh herbal notes, while warmer sites give darker, riper fruit. Oak ageing adds vanilla and spice, with coconut and dill from American oak, and bottle age brings tobacco, leather and dried fruit.

## Styles and ageing
Styles range from fruity young wines to long-aged Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva bottlings, defined by minimum time in oak and bottle. It is often blended with Garnacha, Graciano or Cabernet Sauvignon, and is a key grape in Portuguese port and Douro table wines. Good examples age gracefully for a decade or more.
