## Overview
Gewürztraminer is one of the most instantly recognisable white grapes, known for its exuberant perfume. Its skins are pinkish, and it often gives full-bodied wines with a soft, oily texture.

## Origin and history
It is a pink-skinned, strongly aromatic (musqué) mutation of Savagnin, also called Traminer, an ancient variety that is a parent of many others, including Grüner Veltliner and Sauvignon Blanc. The name Traminer refers to the village of Tramin in Alto Adige, but there is no proof that the variety originated there; north-east France and south-west Germany are also proposed. "Gewürz" means spice in German. Alsace is its best-known home, and it is also grown in Germany, Austria, Alto Adige, eastern Europe, New Zealand and the United States.

## Flavour and structure
Lychee and rose are the classic aromas, often joined by ginger, grapefruit and exotic spice. Acidity is naturally low to medium-low, and alcohol can be high, so wines taste rich and heavy. Cool climates and early picking preserve freshness, while warmer sites give fatter, more tropical wines.

## Styles and ageing
Wines range from dry to sweet. Alsace produces dry and off-dry examples, as well as late-harvest Vendange Tardive and Sélection de Grains Nobles. Most are made without oak. Good examples can age for ten years or more, deepening toward honeyed, spicy and gingerbread notes.
