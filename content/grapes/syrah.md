## Overview
Syrah, known as Shiraz in Australia and parts of the New World, is a dark-skinned red grape that makes deeply coloured, structured wines. It is the only red grape permitted in Northern Rhône appellations such as Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie.

## Origin and history
Despite old legends linking it to Persia or Syracuse, DNA profiling in 1998 by Carole Meredith's team at UC Davis, using material from Montpellier, showed Syrah is a natural cross of two obscure French varieties: Dureza, from the Ardèche, and Mondeuse Blanche, from Savoie. James Busby brought it to Australia in the early 1830s, where it was long called Hermitage and became the country's signature red under the name Shiraz.

## Flavour and structure
Syrah shows blackberry, blueberry, violet and black pepper, with olive, smoke and cured meat notes in cooler climates. Warmer climates give riper, jammier fruit, sweet spice and higher alcohol. Tannin is medium to high, acidity medium and body full.

## Styles and ageing
Cool-climate Syrah, such as Côte-Rôtie, is peppery, floral and elegant. Warm-climate Shiraz, such as from the Barossa Valley, is richer and more opulent. It is often blended, for example with Grenache and Mourvèdre in southern France and Australia, or with a little Viognier in Côte-Rôtie. Quality wines can age for 10 to 20 years, gaining leather, truffle and game notes.
