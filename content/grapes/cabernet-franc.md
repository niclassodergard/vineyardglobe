## Overview
Cabernet Franc is a fragrant red grape that is lighter and more aromatic than its offspring Cabernet Sauvignon. It is a key blending grape in Bordeaux and the main red grape of the Loire Valley.

## Origin and history
Cabernet Franc is believed to have originated in south-west France, possibly in the Basque Country. It is a parent of Cabernet Sauvignon (crossed with Sauvignon Blanc), of Merlot and of Carmenère, which makes it one of the most influential varieties in wine. It has been grown in the Loire for centuries, where it is often called Breton. Plantings have also expanded in Tuscany, Hungary, California, Argentina and Canada.

## Flavour and structure
Cabernet Franc ripens earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon and copes better with cooler climates. Wines show raspberry, red currant and violet, often with a leafy or green pepper note and a pencil-shaving or graphite character. Acidity is medium-high and tannin moderate, so wines feel fresher and more elegant. Riper sites give darker, plusher fruit.

## Styles and ageing
In the Loire it makes wines in appellations such as Chinon, Bourgueil and Saumur-Champigny, as well as rosé. In Saint-Émilion it is blended with Merlot, and Château Cheval Blanc is famous for a high proportion of it. Wines can age well, developing tobacco and earthy notes.
