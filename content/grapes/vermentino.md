## Overview
Vermentino is a Mediterranean white grape that makes fresh, aromatic, often saline wines. It is especially associated with Sardinia, Liguria, Tuscany's coast and Corsica, and with Provence and Languedoc under the name Rolle.

## Origin and history
The origin is uncertain, with Liguria, Corsica and Sardinia all suggested, and one of the earliest documented mentions comes from Piedmont in the seventeenth century. DNA typing indicates that Vermentino is the same variety as Pigato in Liguria and Favorita in Piedmont, with differences in the wines attributed to clonal variation and site, although some sources still treat them as distinct local biotypes. Rolle, its name in Provence and the Languedoc, was long debated but is now generally listed as a synonym.

## Flavour and structure
Vermentino is typically lime, green apple and white peach, with white flowers, wild herbs and a bitter almond finish. Acidity is medium-high and the body medium. Warmer sites yield riper, rounder fruit, while coastal sites show a salty, tangy edge.

## Styles and ageing
Most wines are unoaked, dry and made to drink young, in stainless steel. Richer versions come from lees contact, old vines or some oak, and the grape is also used in Provence blends and rosé. Top examples can age for several years, gaining waxy, nutty notes.
