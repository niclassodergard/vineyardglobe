## Overview
Roussanne is an aromatic, fine-textured white grape of the Rhône. It is prized for perfumed wines with fresher acidity than its usual partner Marsanne and for its ageing potential, yet it is demanding in the vineyard.

## Origin and history
Roussanne is believed to come from the northern Rhône, and its name is usually linked to the russet colour of its ripe berries. DNA work points to a parent and offspring relationship with Marsanne, with the direction unresolved. It is also grown in Savoie under the name Bergeron, notably in Chignin-Bergeron, and has spread to the southern Rhône, the Languedoc, California, Australia and elsewhere.

## Flavour and structure
Roussanne shows pear, herbal tea, honeysuckle and apricot, with nutty and spiced notes as it matures. Acidity is moderate, usually higher than Marsanne's, and the body is medium to full. It ripens late, is prone to rot and powdery mildew and gives irregular yields, and in warm climates it can lose acidity and become heavy.

## Styles and ageing
Wines are dry and textured, from stainless steel to barrel-fermented versions. It is often blended with Marsanne in Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage and Saint-Joseph, and it is one of the white grapes of Châteauneuf-du-Pape, where a few producers bottle it on its own. Roussanne can age gracefully for a decade or more, developing honey and nut complexity.
