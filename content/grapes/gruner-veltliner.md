## Overview
Grüner Veltliner is Austria's most widely planted grape and its signature white. It makes crisp, food-friendly wines with a distinctive peppery character.

## Origin and history
DNA analysis has shown that Grüner Veltliner is a natural cross between Traminer (Savagnin) and a rare variety known from a single ancient vine found around 2000 in St. Georgen am Leithagebirge, in Burgenland, now called St. Georgener-Rebe; the parentage was confirmed in 2007. Despite the name, it is unrelated to Roter Veltliner or Frühroter Veltliner. It is grown mainly in Lower Austria, in Wachau, Kamptal, Kremstal and Weinviertel, and also in neighbouring Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary.

## Flavour and structure
Typical flavours include green apple, citrus and white pepper, with herbal notes of lentil or celery. The pepper character is linked to rotundone, the same compound that gives pepper notes to Syrah. Acidity is medium-high and the body medium, with riper wines showing stone-fruit and a richer texture.

## Styles and ageing
Simple Grüner Veltliner is light, zesty and made to be drunk young, often under screwcap. Wachau and Kamptal examples from the best vineyards, grown on loess or primary rock soils, are fuller and more concentrated. They can age for ten years or more, taking on honeyed, nutty and smoky character. Sparkling and sweet versions are also made.
