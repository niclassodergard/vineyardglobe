## Overview
Nerello Mascalese is Sicily's great red grape of Mount Etna. Its pale, perfumed, high-acid, tannic wines are often compared with Pinot Noir and Nebbiolo.

## Origin and history
The grape takes its name from the Mascali plain on the eastern slopes of Etna. DNA studies first showed a close relationship with Sangiovese, and later parentage analysis identified it as a natural cross of Sangiovese and Mantonico Bianco, an old white grape from Calabria. It has long been grown on Etna's terraced volcanic slopes, often as old, ungrafted bush vines.

## Flavour and structure
Nerello Mascalese is light in colour, with red cherry, wild strawberry, rose, blood orange, herbs and smoke, plus a distinct mineral character from volcanic soils. Acidity is high and tannins medium to high, with a medium body. Altitude, which can reach about 1,000 metres, and lava flows of different ages create marked differences between the vineyard districts (contrade).

## Styles and ageing
Etna Rosso DOC requires at least 80 percent Nerello Mascalese, usually completed with Nerello Cappuccio. It is also made as a rosato and in sparkling wines. Quality wines can age well for a decade or more, developing tobacco, dried fruit and savoury complexity.
