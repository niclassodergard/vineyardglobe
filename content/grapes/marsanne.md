## Overview
Marsanne is a full-bodied, low-acid white grape from the northern Rhône. It is the most planted white variety in Hermitage and a key part of the white wines of Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph and Saint-Péray.

## Origin and history
Marsanne most likely originated in the northern Rhône, and its name is generally linked to the village of Marsanne in the Drôme. DNA work has pointed to a parent and offspring relationship with Roussanne, although the direction is unclear. It was introduced to Switzerland, where it has been called Ermitage, and has since spread to Australia, notably Victoria, and to California.

## Flavour and structure
Young Marsanne is subtle, with almond, marzipan, pear, quince and white flowers. Acidity is medium to low and the texture is rich, oily and weighty. Warm climates can produce flabby wines, so site and yield matter, and cooler sites give more freshness and definition.

## Styles and ageing
It is made as dry, textured whites, sometimes with barrel fermentation, and is frequently blended with Roussanne in the Rhône and with Viognier. Sweet wines are also made in some places. Good examples age well for 10 years or more, gaining honey, nutty and waxy notes.
