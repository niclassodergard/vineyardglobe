## Overview
Silvaner is a white grape that makes subtle, earthy and food-friendly wines. It is a specialty of Franken in Germany, and it is also grown in Rheinhessen, Alsace and the Alto Adige.

## Origin and history
DNA analysis indicates Silvaner is a natural cross of Traminer (Savagnin) and Österreichisch Weiss, an old Austrian variety. This points to an origin in Austria rather than the older belief in Transylvania. The earliest documented planting in Germany was in 1659 at Castell in Franken, from Austrian cuttings. It was Germany's most widely planted grape until Müller-Thurgau overtook it around 1970, and it has since lost much ground. In Switzerland's Valais it is called Johannisberg.

## Flavour and structure
Silvaner is generally neutral and restrained, with green apple, pear, citrus, herbal and hay notes, and often a mineral or earthy quality. Acidity is medium, body medium and tannin absent. Site matters greatly: limestone and gypsum soils in Franken give more depth and texture, while higher yields give simple, light wines.

## Styles and ageing
Most Silvaner is dry and fresh, and drinks well young. Franken wines are traditionally bottled in the flattened Bocksbeutel, and the best dry Silvaners from leading Franken estates are labelled Grosses Gewächs. Careful, low-yielding Silvaner can age for a decade or more, gaining nutty and honeyed notes. It is also made in off-dry and sweet styles.
