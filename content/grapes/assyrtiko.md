## Overview
Assyrtiko is Greece's most celebrated white grape. It comes from the island of Santorini, and it is prized for retaining high acidity even in a hot climate, giving taut, mineral and long-lived wines.

## Origin and history
Assyrtiko is regarded as native to Santorini, where vines have been cultivated for centuries. The origin of its name is uncertain. Phylloxera has never established itself on Santorini, so its vines are ungrafted and many root systems are very old. This is usually credited to the sandy, clay-poor volcanic soils, though the exact reasons are still debated. Growers train vines into low baskets called kouloura, which shelter the grapes from strong winds and sun. The grape has also been planted in mainland Greece and in some other countries.

## Flavour and structure
Assyrtiko shows lemon, grapefruit and lime, with wet stone, sea spray and smoky notes. It keeps high acidity and moderate pH even at high sugar ripeness, and alcohol is often fairly high. Body is medium to full, and there is a distinct saline, mineral quality on volcanic soils.

## Styles and ageing
Most Assyrtiko is made as a dry, unoaked white, but barrel-fermented versions exist. Santorini is also known for Vinsanto, a sweet wine from sun-dried grapes. Quality dry wines can age for ten years or more, gaining honeyed, waxy notes while keeping their acidity.
