## Overview
Chenin Blanc is a versatile white grape with naturally high acidity, able to make dry, sweet and sparkling wines of great range. It is perhaps the most stylistically varied of the classic white grapes.

## Origin and history
Chenin Blanc is from the Loire Valley, in Anjou and Touraine. Claims that it grew at the abbey of Glanfeuil in Anjou in the ninth century are unproven; firm written records begin in the 16th century, including Rabelais in 1534. Its name is linked to Mont-Chenin in Touraine. DNA analysis suggests it is an offspring of Savagnin (Traminer), which makes it a close relative of Sauvignon Blanc. It travelled to South Africa in the seventeenth century, where it is known as Steen and is the most widely planted variety. It is also grown in California, Australia and Argentina.

## Flavour and structure
Typical flavours are quince, green apple, pear and lemon, developing honey, chamomile and wax with age or noble rot. High acidity gives freshness and ageing power, while body ranges from light to full depending on ripeness and winemaking. Yields strongly affect quality.

## Styles and ageing
In the Loire it makes dry Savennières and Vouvray, off-dry and sweet wines such as Vouvray moelleux, Coteaux du Layon and Quarts de Chaume, and sparkling wines. South Africa produces fruity and oaked styles. Good examples, especially sweet ones, can age for decades.
