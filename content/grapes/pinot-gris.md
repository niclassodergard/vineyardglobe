## Overview
Pinot Gris is a grey-pink skinned grape that makes white wines in a wide range of styles. It is known as Pinot Grigio in Italy, where it usually makes light, crisp wines, and is richer and more textured in Alsace.

## Origin and history
Pinot Gris is a colour mutation of Pinot, the same variety as Pinot Noir and Pinot Blanc, and it has long been grown in Burgundy. It spread to Alsace, Germany, northern Italy and later Oregon and New Zealand. In Germany it was propagated from 1711 by Johann Seger Ruland of Speyer, hence the name Ruländer, now used mainly for sweeter wines, while Grauburgunder is used for dry ones.

## Flavour and structure
Flavours include pear, apple, lemon and almond, with peach, honey and ginger in riper examples. Acidity is moderate and in warm sites it can be low. Alsace wines are fuller and often show a slightly oily texture, while Italian wines are lighter and more neutral.

## Styles and ageing
Styles range from light, dry, simple wines for early drinking to richer, off-dry examples and sweet late-harvest wines such as Alsace Vendange Tardive and Sélection de Grains Nobles. Most are drunk young, but the best Alsace wines can age well for several years and gain spicy, honeyed complexity.
