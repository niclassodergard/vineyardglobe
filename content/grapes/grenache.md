## Overview
Grenache is one of the world's most widely planted red grapes. It thrives in hot, dry conditions and is the backbone of many Mediterranean red wines and rosés.

## Origin and history
Grenache is generally believed to originate in Spain, probably in Aragon, where it is called Garnacha. Some Sardinian researchers argue that it began there as Cannonau, but most authorities still favour a Spanish origin. It spread across the western Mediterranean, including to southern France, where it is called Grenache. It was later planted widely in Australia, California and elsewhere. It is a variety that buds early and ripens late, and it needs warmth and sunshine to ripen fully.

## Flavour and structure
Grenache gives ripe red fruit, such as strawberry and raspberry, with dried herbs, white pepper and spice. It has relatively pale colour, low to moderate tannin and acidity, and often high alcohol. Old bush vines yield concentrated, more structured wines.

## Styles and ageing
It is the main grape in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, often blended with Syrah and Mourvèdre, a key component of Priorat and Côtes du Rhône, and a supporting grape in Rioja. It also makes dry rosés, such as those of Tavel and Provence, and fortified sweet wines such as Banyuls and Rivesaltes. Most wines are best within a few years, but top examples from old vines can age for a decade or more.
