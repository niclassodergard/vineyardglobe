## Overview
Riesling is a highly aromatic white grape prized for its high acidity and its ability to show the character of the vineyard. It makes wines from bone dry to lusciously sweet and is among the longest-lived of all white wines.

## Origin and history
Riesling originated in the Rhine region of Germany, and the earliest known record dates from 1435, at Rüsselsheim. DNA analysis shows Gouais Blanc as one parent; the other appears to be a cross of Traminer with a wild vine. It remains Germany's signature grape, and is also important in Alsace, Austria, Australia's Clare and Eden Valleys, and Washington State.

## Flavour and structure
Cool climates give lime, green apple and floral notes with piercing acidity. Warmer sites give peach and apricot. Riesling is often fairly low in alcohol, especially in Germany, light to medium in body and rarely shows oak flavour, though large old casks are traditional. With age it develops honey, toast and a petrol-like note.

## Styles and ageing
Styles include dry wines, off-dry wines and sweet wines from late-harvest grapes, botrytis or frozen grapes (Eiswein). German wines are classified by ripeness at harvest, from Kabinett to Trockenbeerenauslese. Thanks to acidity and sugar, quality wines can age for decades.
