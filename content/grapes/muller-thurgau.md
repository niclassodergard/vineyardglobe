## Overview
Müller-Thurgau is a white grape that makes soft, fragrant, easy-drinking wines. It was Germany's most planted variety for much of the late 20th century, and it is still widely grown in Germany, Austria, northern Italy and central Europe.

## Origin and history
It was bred in 1882 by Hermann Müller, from the Swiss canton of Thurgau, at the Geisenheim research institute. For over a century it was thought to be a Riesling × Silvaner cross, hence its Swiss name Riesling-Sylvaner. DNA analysis published in 2000 by Dettweiler and colleagues showed that its parents are in fact Riesling and Madeleine Royale. Its early ripening and high yields made it hugely popular in the 20th century, and it overtook Silvaner as Germany's leading grape around 1970, though its area has since more than halved. It is called Rivaner in Luxembourg and for some dry German wines.

## Flavour and structure
Müller-Thurgau is mild, with peach, apple, elderflower, citrus and a light muscat-like perfume. It has low to medium-low acidity, no tannin and a light to medium body. High yields give neutral wine, while lower yields and cooler sites increase concentration and freshness.

## Styles and ageing
Most examples are dry or off-dry, made to drink young and often in stainless steel. Better wines from Alto Adige and Franken show more depth and minerality. It was also a mainstay of sweetish blends such as Liebfraumilch. It is rarely made for long ageing and is best within two or three years.
